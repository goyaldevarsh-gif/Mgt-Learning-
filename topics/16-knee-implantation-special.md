## 1. Implantation and cementing (Technique 7.11)

:::flow
[teal] Before cement | Defects treated, balance ok, patella tracks [[p.453]]
v
Plug IM canals ;; Perforate sclerotic bone ;; Pulsatile lavage + dry
v
[amber] Tibia first, then femur, then patella | Dough phase, pressurise keel
v
Trial spacer thick enough to hold full extension | Remove ALL debris (third-body wear)
:::

- Cement penetration **2-5 mm** is enough [[p.453]]
- Use little cement **posteriorly** (hard to clean)
- **Cementless:** apposition must be perfect: **gaps > 0.5 mm fill with fibrous tissue**; ingrowth is maximal around **screws/pegs** [[p.454]]
- **Closure:** femoral IM plug cuts blood loss **20-25%**; flex **> 90 deg** to check tracking; skin closed at ~**40 deg** flexion. Drains not routine (authors)

> [!samajh] Cementing ka rule
> **Dry surface + dough phase + pressurise + debris-free.** Thin trial spacer = hyperextension aur tibial tray peeche se uth sakti hai.

## 2. UKA and PFA technique

- UKA selection: **intact ACL, unicompartmental arthritis, correctable deformity, reasonable weight**; modern UKA **82-98% at 10 yrs** [[p.454]]
- **Remove osteophytes first** (Figs 7.62-7.63): often corrects deformity and confirms UKA suitability
- Fixed bearing: **tibia cut first**, extension spacer sets femoral cut; avoid overstuffing
- Mobile bearing: strict gap equalisation or bearing "spits out"

![Fig 7.62: Medial osteophytes tent the MCL and block correction of varus](figs/f7_62.jpg)

![Fig 7.63: After osteophyte resection, the knee passively corrects](figs/f7_63.jpg)

- PFA: **>= 70% survival at 8-10 yrs**; conversion to TKA commoner than after UKA; commonest reoperation = tibiofemoral arthritis progression [[p.455-456]]

## 3. Post-op

- Compressive dressing, immobilizer until quads safe, ROM with or without **CPM** [[p.456]]
- **CPM:** faster flexion, **no proven effect** on DVT, long-term ROM or function
- Extension: foot on pillow. Flexion: dangle legs. Pre-op PT teaching helps

## 4. TKA after HTO

| Problem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Revision risk | **8% at 6 yrs** in 166 TKAs; male, obese, young, laxity, malalignment |
| Scars | Transverse ignore; **lateral longitudinal: 8 cm skin bridge** |
| Patella baja / eversion | Lateral release, V-Y quadricepsplasty or TTO |
| Balancing | Many use **routine PCL substitution** |
| Failed valgus closing wedge | Lateral tibial defect (Fig 7.64); **medially offset canal**: EM guide, offset stem, avoid tray IR |

![Fig 7.64: Lateral tibial bone deficiency after high tibial osteotomy](figs/f7_64.jpg)

## 5. After patellectomy and Charcot

- **PS did better than CR** (CR had more AP instability) [[p.457]]
- Reason: patellectomy breaks the **four-bar linkage** (quad tendon, patellar tendon, cruciates): moment arm shrinks, more posterior tibial force

![Fig 7.66: Four-bar linkage of the cruciates; loss of patella changes extensor moment arm](figs/f7_66.jpg)

- **Charcot knee:** relative contraindication; use revision-type components or **rotating hinge** + brace, since early dislocation/instability is common [[p.458]]

## 6. Hemophilia, HIV, diabetes, psoriasis

| Condition | Key point |
|---|---|
| Hemophilia | Keep **factor VIII at 100%** (< 80% = more complications) |
| HIV | Infection **7.7% vs 3.3%**; needs ID clearance (viral load, CD4) |
| Diabetes | More wound problems and infection; with metabolic syndrome up to **10 x** complications |
| Psoriasis | ~7% inflammatory arthritis; plaques harbour bacteria, control skin first |

> [!samajh] Teeno ka theme **infection**. Optimise pehle (factor, viral load, HbA1c, skin).

![Fig 7.67: TKA in hemophilic arthropathy with medial tibial structural autograft and press-fit stems](figs/f7_67.jpg)
