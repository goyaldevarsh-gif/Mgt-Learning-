## Big picture

Pain around the coccyx that is **worse on sitting, especially when leaning back or rising from a chair**, and relieved on standing.

> [!samajh] Baithne par tailbone zameen ki taraf dabti hai. Isliye "sit-to-stand" mein sabse zyada dard, aur khade hone par aaram.

## Causes {revise}

- Trauma: a fall on the buttocks, childbirth.
- **Obesity**: sitting tilts the pelvis backward, which loads the coccyx. Very thin people lack padding.
- Hypermobility or subluxation of the sacrococcygeal or intercoccygeal joint.
- Coccygeal spicule, degenerative change, idiopathic.
- Referred causes to rule out: pilonidal disease, rectal, pelvic or lumbar pathology, tumour (rare).

## Examination

- Direct tenderness over the coccyx.
- **Per rectal exam** with a gloved finger and external thumb: tenderness, mobility, a spicule. Pain on gentle movement of the coccyx supports the diagnosis.
- Check the lumbar spine, hips, skin and sacral area for pilonidal sinus.

> [!verify] Many clinicians do a per rectal exam; confirm your department's practice and consent.

## Imaging

- **Dynamic lateral X-rays**: sitting and standing. The aim is to see how much the coccyx moves when you sit.
- Maigne's criteria for instability, roughly: **posterior luxation** of the coccyx or **flexion of about 25 degrees or more** between sitting and standing. A spicule is also noted.
- MRI if you suspect a tumour or other cause.

> [!verify] Exact degree threshold (about 25 degrees) and the Postacchini and Massobrio type I to IV or V classification of coccyx shape. Confirm numbers and number of types in your textbook.

## Treatment ladder {revise}

:::flow
[teal] Conservative: weeks to months
v
Cushion (ring or wedge, cut-out), posture, NSAIDs, short course
v
[amber] Local injection (steroid + local anaesthetic) / ganglion impar block / manipulation
v
[red] Coccygectomy: last resort, after long failed conservative care
:::

1. **Reassure**: most settle within weeks to months.
2. **Wedge or ring cushion**, lean forward when sitting, avoid prolonged hard seats.
3. NSAIDs, heat, physiotherapy including pelvic floor relaxation.
4. Injection: local steroid around the coccyx, or ganglion impar block.
5. **Coccygectomy** if persistent despite 6 months or more of care and with a clear mobile or unstable coccyx. Higher success in the instability pattern. Wound infection and delayed healing are known problems.

> [!verify] Duration of failed conservative care before surgery (commonly several months) and expected success rate.

## Viva {viva}

:::qa Which position worsens the pain?
Sitting, especially leaning backward, and rising from sitting.
:::

:::qa Why does obesity cause coccydynia?
Sitting tilts the pelvis backward so the coccyx takes the load.
:::

:::qa Which X-ray do you order?
Dynamic lateral views in sitting and standing.
:::
