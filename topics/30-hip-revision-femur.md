## 1. Classification

### AAOS femoral (D'Antonio) [[p.311-313]]
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I | **Segmental** (cortical shell lost): proximal partial/complete, intercalary, greater trochanter |
| II | **Cavitary** (contained): cancellous, cortical, **ectasia** (dilated + thin cortex) |
| III | Combined |
| IV | Malalignment (rotational, angular) |
| V | Stenosis |
| VI | Discontinuity (fracture / nonunion) |

- Levels: **I** above the lower border of lesser trochanter; **II** from there to 10 cm distal; **III** further distal

![Fig 3.179: Femoral defects: segmental, cavitary, combined, malalignment, stenosis, discontinuity, levels](figs/h3_179.jpg)

### Della Valle and Paprosky [[p.312, 314]]
| Type | Defect | Stem choice |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Minimal metaphyseal loss, diaphysis intact (e.g. smooth press-fit stem) | Primary-length cemented or cementless |
| **II** | Extensive metaphyseal loss to lesser trochanter, diaphysis intact | **Calcar replacement**; extensively coated, tapered fluted, proximal-porous modular |
| **IIIA** | Metaphysis nonsupportive, **> 4 cm** isthmus available | Extensively coated (8 inch / 20 cm or longer), tapered fluted |
| **IIIB** | Metaphysis nonsupportive, **< 4 cm** isthmus | **Impaction grafting** + tapered fluted (cylindrical extensively coated stems perform poorly) |
| **IV** | Widened canal, no diaphyseal support | Impaction grafting, **allograft-prosthesis composite**, or modular tumour prosthesis |

![Fig 3.180: Femoral deficiency types I-IV with isthmus available for distal fixation](figs/h3_180.jpg)

> [!samajh] Paprosky femur ek line me
> **Isthmus kitna bacha hai?** > 4 cm = distal fixation (IIIA). < 4 cm = graft/fluted (IIIB). Kuch nahi = composite ya megaprosthesis (IV).

- Objectives [[p.312]]: preserve integrity and bone stock, rigid fixation, restore biomechanics, equal leg length

## 2. Segmental defects [[p.312-315]]

- Stress returns to normal **2 bone diameters** beyond a defect, so stem must pass that
- Hole **< 30%** of diameter: strength loss similar regardless of size; **< 1/3** use particulate graft; larger window: **onlay cortical strut** (contour endosteal surface to the femur, multiple cerclage; union **96%**, Lim)
- Missing medial neck/lesser trochanter: **calcar replacement or extended neck stem** restores length; rotational stability from cement, flutes, extensive porous coat or curve
- Trochanteric fragment is hard to attach on a calcar stem, so use **ETO** if osteotomy needed
- Defect extends below LT (zones II-III): **distal fixation required**, strut grafts cannot give primary support

![Fig 3.182: Cortical strut: contour to match, fix with multiple cerclage wires](figs/h3_182.jpg)

## 3. Cavitary defects [[p.315-316]]

- **Cemented revision** is weak: bone surface smooth after loosening. Mulroy/Harris: 20% re-revised by 15 yrs (+6% loose). Katz 26%. Hultmark **85% at 10 yrs**. Cement shear strength after revision: **20.6%** of primary, **6.8%** after second (Dohmae)
- Particulate graft only fills; **cannot** give axial or rotational stability
- Proximal-distal size mismatch (ectasia): ream larger (wastes bone, stress shielding, thigh pain), **oversize-proximal revision stem**, distal fixation + graft or reduction osteotomy, or impaction grafting
- **Modular (Cameron, Technique 3.30):** separate proximal sleeve and distal stem; aseptic loosening **1.4%** (320 hips, 7 yrs); HA sleeve **90.5% at 15 yrs** in types I-IIIA; reliable in II, IIIA but not IIIB/IV

## 4. Choosing stem by situation

:::flow
[teal] Extensively porous-coated (Technique 3.31, Mallory/Head) | Distal fixation, **~2/3 proximal body supported by host bone**; bowed stem: flexible reamers, ream 1 mm over; straight: line-to-line. Failure **4.1% at 14 yrs** (Weeden/Paprosky); fails if < 4 cm isthmus [[p.316-317]]
v
Tapered fluted modular (Technique 3.32, Kwong) | Types III-IV; 143 pts: **97%**, 2.1 mm subsidence; 96% at 10 yrs; taper failure: add cable/strut. Combine with **ETO** for straight access; **reduction osteotomy** (longitudinal wedge) narrows proximal femur [[p.317]]
v
[amber] Impaction grafting + cemented (Technique 3.33, Gie) | Segmental defects meshed; cement plug; pack chips with graded packers, tamp until rotationally stable; **98.8% (aseptic loosening) and 87.7% (revision) at 20 yrs** (705 hips); Swedish 94% at 15 yrs. Complications: **fracture 5.4%, dislocation 4.1%** [[p.317-318]]
:::

## 5. Massive deficits [[p.318-321]]

### Allograft-prosthesis composite (Technique 3.34)
- Large segment from a tissue bank; **radiographic calibration markers** to match length
- Two teams (prep graft on a side table); transtrochanteric, **large trochanteric fragment** for reattachment; mark **pin in pelvis** to restore length
- Host osteotomy: **step-cut/oblique**; match rotation by **linea aspera**; trial in graft
- **Cement the stem into the graft** (no ingrowth expected), **not into the host** if press-fit possible; clear cement from graft-host junction; stability by step-cut, press-fit, plate or strut + cerclage
- Wrap residual host femur around junction as vascular envelope
- Problems: **dislocation high** (abduction orthosis/spica), fracture of unsupported graft, **infection** (large dead bone; antibiotic cement), union slow, **delay weight bearing till union**
- Results: Sternheim **75% at 15 yrs** (28); Babis 69% at 12 yrs

![Fig 3.185: Proximal femoral allograft with step-cut and cemented stem](figs/h3_185.jpg)

### Modular megaprosthesis (Technique 3.35)
- For elderly, low-demand, severely compromised femur; proximal porous coating helps soft-tissue attachment
- Meta-analysis (356 hips, 3.8 yrs): retention **83%**, reoperation 23%, **instability 16%**. Paprosky IIIB/IV series: **86% at 5 yrs, 66% at 10 yrs**
- Constrained liner may be needed; wrap remaining bone around prosthesis for soft-tissue control

![Fig 3.186: Modular proximal femoral replacement](figs/h3_186.jpg)

- **Deformity** (varus, anterior bow): **transverse femoral osteotomy** and extensively coated stem for axial + rotational stability (Fig 3.187) [[p.321]]

:::qa Femur me IIIA aur IIIB ka farq?
Both have non-supportive metaphysis. IIIA > 4 cm isthmus (distal fixation with long extensively coated or fluted stem). IIIB < 4 cm (impaction grafting or fluted tapered) [[p.312]].
:::

:::qa Strut graft ke baare me 2 rules?
Stem must bypass the defect by 2 bone diameters; strut cannot provide primary support for the stem, and is fixed with multiple cerclage wires [[p.312, 315]].
:::
