## 1. Indications [[p.286-287]]

- Revisions were **17.5%** of US hip arthroplasties in 1990-2002 (predicted 14.5% by 2030)
- Revision = more blood loss and time; more infection, VTE, dislocation, nerve palsy, fracture
- **First rule:** is the pain really from the THA? Exclude spine, tumour, vascular, stress fracture, CRPS
- **Commonest indications (NIS):** dislocation, mechanical loosening, other mechanical, infection, osteolysis, periprosthetic fracture, wear, breakage
- **Revise for:** disabling pain from loosening; progressive bone loss even if painless; **progressive stem deformation or incomplete fracture** (otherwise full fracture makes distal fragment hard to remove); complete stem fracture; Morse taper failure; liner lock failure
- **Do NOT revise for:** painless stiffness, limb lengthening alone (unless significant HO), limp without mechanical cause. Shortening can cause dislocation
- Pain same as pre-op and never relieved: suspect **infection or technical problem**; if no source found, do not operate to "do something"
- Routine pre-revision aspiration abandoned (poor sensitivity/PPV); aspirate only on clinical/lab suspicion
- Frail: modified **Girdlestone** may be better
- Loose acetabulum = **groin**; loose stem = **thigh or knee** [[p.287]]

## 2. Pre-op planning [[p.287-288]]

- Good X-rays of **whole pelvis and entire femur** (thin cortex hidden by cement), magnification marker, lateral femur for bow
- **CT** for acetabular bone (even with metal back); vascular study if intrapelvic cement or markedly protruded cup (Fig 3.116 in book is referred)
- Identify implant from X-ray/op note; **system-specific extraction tools**, head sizes and liners (may avoid revising both)
- Remember: 70-75 mm cups, calcar-replacing, long-stem, extended necks, custom triflange for discontinuity (Fig 3.145)

## 3. Approach [[p.288-289]]

| Approach | Comment |
|---|---|
| Use old incision | Skin necrosis between incisions less than knee, but think |
| Anterior / anterolateral / direct lateral / posterolateral | Straightforward cup revisions. Anterior: poor posterior column + femur; **superior gluteal nerve** at risk if > 5 cm above acetabular rim |
| **Posterolateral + lift vastus lateralis** | Best posterior column and shaft; **higher dislocation risk**; anterior exposure hard with fixed-head stem |
| **Transtrochanteric** | Most complete exposure of both sides; trochanter reattachment problems (osteoporotic, leg lengthened) |
| **Trochanteric slide** | Preferred if standard fragment enough; **keep vastus attached** (stops proximal migration) |
| **Extended trochanteric osteotomy (ETO)** | Anticipated stem/cement removal trouble |

![Fig 3.146: Extended trochanteric osteotomy relative to gluteus medius, minimus and vastus lateralis](figs/h3_146.jpg)

- **Technique 3.10 (transtrochanteric):** use an existing trochanteric nonunion for exposure; tag pseudocapsule/short rotators; protect sciatic nerve; vastus lifted posterior to anterior; osteotomy posterior to anterior with saw, anterior cortex left, finish with osteotome [[p.289]]

## 4. Removing the femoral stem [[p.289-294]]

:::flow
[teal] Cemented stem (Technique 3.11) | Extractor or collar as driving platform; remove greater trochanter overhang and lateral cement over shoulder; precoated stems: burr off cement or coating **before** osteotomes [[p.289-290]]
v
Fails? | ETO or small window **just distal to the cement exposed**, not at stem tip
:::

:::flow
[teal] Cementless stem (Technique 3.12) | Thin flexible osteotomes (standard ones split the femur) or thin burr around proximal porous area [[p.290-291]]
v
[amber] Extensive distal ingrowth (Technique 3.13, Glassman and Engh) | Small window at junction of triangular and cylindrical part, **cut stem with carbide burr**, remove proximal part, **trephine reamer** over distal part [[p.291-292]]
:::

- **Do not use osteotomes on cement before stem is out** (wedge effect splits femur)
- Modular head stems: standard extractors just pull off head, so use the **system's own extractor**
- **ETO (Technique 3.14, Younger):** trochanter + lateral femoral cortex (~1/3 circumference) as one piece with vastus attached; hinged anteriorly. Isthmus left for **5-6 cm** endosteal contact of new stem; **prophylactic cable distal to osteotomy**; repair with multiple cerclage; advance distally to tension abductors; graft posterior edge. Union **98-99%**. **Best with cementless revision** (cement blocks union). Also: varus remodeling, loose stem in good cement, hard dislocation [[p.292-294]]

### Broken stem [[p.294-295]]
- Proximal fragment usually loose; distal fragment fixed. Fibre-optic light to see
- **Proximal window (Technique 3.15, Moreland):** small window 4 x 10 mm **just distal to the fractured end**, carbide punch driven proximally; successful in 10/10
- **Distal window (3.16):** 0.8 cm window, extends ~3 cm either side of stem tip; offset driver; replace cortical piece with cerclage; new stem must bypass by **2 cortical diameters**; last resort
- Broken cementless stem: disrupt ingrowth, **trephine** the distal part

## 5. Removing femoral cement [[p.295-298]]

- Hardest and most hazardous part; done **after cup revision** (bleeding from canal blocks view)
- Tools: fibre-optic, long suction, cement osteotomes, pituitary rongeur, hooks, long burr, graduated reamers, C-arm
- **Proximal (3.17):** clear trochanteric overhang first (especially after varus stem, else instruments exit posterolaterally, Fig 3.155). **Radial splits first**, then osteotomes to push fragments into the centre (Fig 3.156-157). Wire thin cortex
- **Distal plug:** thin plug can be driven distally and left (**not if infected**); hook between cortex and plug, turn 90 deg (Fig 3.158); solid plug: **centre-drill with sleeve**, masonry drills, reverse hook or tap
- **High-speed burr (3.18, Turner):** biplane C-arm always; cuts on forward stroke; irrigate (hot burr re-melts cement); centre of canal, not old stem path. **Perforation 10%** (Turner). Sudden loss of resistance = perforation: ball-tip guidewire, X-ray; fix with window, strut graft, or long stem past by 2 cortical diameters
- **Controlled perforations (3.19, Mallory):** 9 mm hole anterior/anterolateral cortex 8-10 cm below vastus ridge; extra holes **>= 5 cm apart**; stem past the most distal hole by **twice the femoral diameter**. Strain rises 40% for a **lateral** hole, so avoid lateral
- Ultrasonic tools: low perforation, best for cement below the tip; need hand tools too
- **If recementing:** leave well-bonded cement without lucency/infection; new cement bonds to old. **If cementless:** remove all cement (graft + biologic fixation)
