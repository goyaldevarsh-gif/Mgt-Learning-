## 1. Cup removal [[p.298-301]]

| Cup | Approach |
|---|---|
| **All-poly cemented** (3.20) | Remove flange; thin osteotomes between cup and cement (**never between cement and bone**); pry out; or **quarter it** with saw/burr; or ream the poly thin (de Thomasson) |
| **Metal-backed cemented** (3.21) | Fragment cement, tap out; else remove liner, **cut a quarter of metal back** with carbide burr. Displaced into pelvis: **imaging, drape for abdominal incision, vascular surgeon** |
| **Well-fixed cementless** (3.22, Mitchell) | **Explant system**: centring head matched to liner, short blade then full-radius blade around the shell; ~5 min, minimal bone loss (31 cases) |

- Cement left in the pelvis is not removed unless **infection** [[p.299]]
- Remove all fibrous membrane (Cobb/curette/burr)
- If revision is only for malposition and recurrent dislocation: **liner with larger lip/offset** may do [[p.300]]

## 2. Classifying acetabular deficiency [[p.300-304]]

### Causes
Osteolysis (wear, loosening, infection); over-resection at previous surgery (esp. resurfacing); pre-existing fracture/dysplasia; bone destroyed during removal.

### AAOS (D'Antonio)
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I | **Segmental** (rim or medial wall lost) |
| II | **Cavitary** (volume loss, rim/wall intact) |
| III | Combined |
| IV | **Pelvic discontinuity** |
| V | Arthrodesis |

Each subdivided: peripheral, superior, anterior, posterior, central.

![Fig 3.166: AAOS defects: segmental, cavitary, combined, discontinuity, arthrodesis](figs/h3_166.jpg)

### Paprosky (X-ray based; predicts intra-op defect)
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **I** | Rim supportive, no lysis, no migration |
| **IIA** | Distorted hemisphere, columns intact, **< 2 cm** superomedial migration |
| **IIB** | < 2 cm superolateral (no dome) |
| **IIC** | Medial only (medial wall breached, rim intact) |
| **IIIA** | **> 2 cm** superior migration; Kohler's line **intact**; 30-60% supported by graft; bone loss 10 to 2 o'clock |
| **IIIB** | > 2 cm; **Kohler's line not intact**; > 60% by graft; bone loss 9 to 5 o'clock |

![Fig 3.167: Paprosky types I, IIA-C, IIIA, IIIB](figs/h3_167.jpg)

> [!samajh] Paprosky yaad rakhne ka tareeka
> **I = kuch nahi. II = hemisphere bigda par columns theek (A upar-andar, B upar-bahar, C sirf andar). III = > 2 cm upar; A me Kohler line intact, B me toot gayi.**

- Work-up: AP, lateral, **Judet views, CT** (at times in presence of metal-backed cup)

## 3. Goals [[p.302]]
Restore **anatomic centre of rotation**, normal mechanics, structural integrity, **rigid fixation**. Most are **cementless** now.

## 4. Management by defect

:::flow
[teal] Cavitary (3.23) | Small: ream a size larger. Large: **morselised graft impacted** with last reamer in reverse; graft is only a filler, **rim must give stability** [[p.302]]
v
Cup | **1-2 mm oversize** for rim fit; screws; **liner for 32 mm+ head**
v
[amber] Segmental small (peripheral, anterior) | Often ignore if cup is contained and centre restored
v
[red] Segmental large (posterior/superior) | Structural graft or **modular metal augment** (needed if hip centre would need > **2.5 cm** superior shift) [[p.303]]
:::

- Structural graft results best if **>= 50% of cup support is host bone**
- **Augment advantages:** rigid initial fixation, no resorption, many sizes. Porous tantalum + augment: good mid-term except in **pelvic discontinuity**
- **Oblong cups:** for superior segmental defects, but tend to be placed too vertical; replaced by augments + hemispherical cup in author's practice
- **Femoral head allograft (3.24):** for superior or posterior rim only. Use **OA head** (not osteoporotic fracture head). Superior: lag screws; **posterior: buttress plate**. Ream after provisional fixation; ancillary screws go cup-to-**host**, not graft
- **Metal augment (3.25, Jenkins):** ream to 2 points of fixation; trial one size up; place cup first with 2 screws, back off, place augment, cement the interface (doughy cement), screw both (superior + inferior hemispheres)
- **Combined defects:** distal femoral allograft, augments, cancellous graft + antiprotrusio cage, acetabular allograft, custom triflange, high hip centre. Hemispherical cups do better with **> 50% host contact**; tantalum may need less
- **Sporer "7" graft (3.26):** distal femoral/proximal tibial allograft, > 3 cm superior migration with columns mostly intact (Paprosky IIIA); 17/23 successful at 10 yrs. Segmental defect reconstructed **first**, cavity filled after

## 5. Pelvic discontinuity [[p.309-312]]

- Transverse fracture, **separation of superior and inferior halves**. Radiation history = low healing potential

:::flow
[teal] Acute or chronic with healing potential | **Posterior column plating** + hemispherical cup (Abdel); compression with structural graft or tantalum cup as plate
v
[red] Poor healing potential (irradiation) | **Distract**, highly porous cup (3.27 Sheth); or **cup-cage**; or **custom triflange**
:::

- **Distraction (3.27):** augments as needed, lamina spreader, pins in dome and ischium with distractor, ream in reverse, cup with multiple screws using ischium and sciatic buttress. **Touch-down WB 6-12 weeks**
- **Cup-cage (3.28):** hemispherical cup + augment, cage over cup, inferior flange in ischium (or half-cage without it), **screw through cage and cup to dome (unitise)**, iliac flange screws
- **Custom triflange (3.29, DeBoer):** 20 hips, 10 yrs, none revised or loose; **dislocation 5/20** commonest problem; trace sciatic nerve to notch. Massive allograft: 45% revised at 5 yrs (better with ring)
- Such cases: refer to high-volume centre

![Fig 3.176: Cup-cage: tantalum cup, superior augment, cage and cemented liner](figs/h3_176.jpg)

:::qa Paprosky IIIA vs IIIB?
Both > 2 cm migration. IIIA: Kohler's line intact, 30-60% graft support, 10-2 o'clock loss. IIIB: Kohler's not intact, > 60% graft support, 9-5 o'clock loss [[p.304]].
:::

:::qa Cup-cage kab?
Pelvic discontinuity / large segmental defects with poor biologic fixation potential; cup unitised with cage by screws through both [[p.311]].
:::
