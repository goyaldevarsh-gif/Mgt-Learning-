## 1. Zones to remember

![Fig 3.134: Gruen zones (femur, 7) and DeLee-Charnley zones (acetabulum, 3)](figs/h3_134.jpg)

- **Femur: Gruen, 7 zones.** Acetabulum: **DeLee and Charnley, 3 zones** [[p.278]]
- Standard limb position: 10 deg rotation fakes migration (Albert)

## 2. Femoral loosening

### Cemented stem: signs [[p.278]]
1. Lucency stem-cement **zone 1** (debonding)
2. Lucency cement-bone
3. **Subsidence** (stem in cement, or stem + mantle)
4. **Varus** shift
5. Cement fragmentation (zone 7)
6. **Cement fracture near tip (zone 4)**
7. Stem deformation
8. Stem fracture

- **Harris:** definite = migration of component or cement; probable = complete lucency around cement; possible = incomplete lucency > 50%
- **Technical causes** (book list): soft cancellous bone left on medial neck, thin mantle, canal over-cleaned (no interdigitation), too little cement or lamination, no pressurisation, stem moved during curing, not centralised, voids

| Barrack cement grade | Description | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| A | Complete fill, "white-out" | Best |
| B | Slight lucency < 50% | OK |
| C | 50-99% lucency or mantle defect | More loosening |
| D | Complete lucency or defect at tip | More loosening |

![Fig 3.135: Cement grade A (white-out) and B](figs/h3_135.jpg)

### Cementless stem [[p.278-280]]
| Engh class | Features |
|---|---|
| **Bone ingrowth** | No subsidence, minimal lines, "spot welds", cortical hypertrophy at distal porous end, calcar atrophy |
| **Stable fibrous** | No migration, parallel radiopaque lines ~1 mm from stem |
| **Unstable** | Progressive subsidence, **divergent** lines, thickening under collar and at tip |

- Early subsidence can still stabilise. Late subsidence = unstable
- **Bony pedestal in zone 4** = pistoning. Progressive **bead shedding** = micromotion

## 3. Acetabular loosening [[p.280-281]]

### Cemented cup
- Signs: bone resorption > 2 mm progressive after 6 months; **cephalad migration + rotation** (correlates best with aseptic loosening); wear; lucency 2 mm
- Loosens by **3-D bone resorption from the periphery toward the dome** (Schmalzried), not at cup-cement interface
- **Loose if radiolucency >= 2 mm in all 3 zones.** Hodgkinson: 3 zones = **94%** loose, 2 zones 71%, 1 zone 7%. Extent matters more than width
- **Change in position = definite loosening** (use teardrop as reference) (Fig 3.139)
- Technical causes: poor support (superior/posterior), medial wall thin or fractured, cartilage and blood left, no keyholes, **poor pressurisation** (cup flanges help), uneven cement (use spacers/pods), cup moved while curing, undersized cup in large mantle, **impingement from malposition**

### Cementless cup (Engh)
- Stable / probably unstable (progressive lucency) / definitely unstable (migration)
- Porous cups rarely loosen by 10 yrs; lucencies common (> 50% of revision cups nonprogressive, Leopold). **Threaded and HA nonporous cups failed early**

![Fig 3.139: Cemented cup migrated proximally and more horizontal = definite loosening](figs/h3_139.jpg)

## 4. Clinical diagnosis [[p.281-282]]

- **Start-up pain** (first steps, then eases) = loose but settling implant
- **Acetabular loose = groin pain. Femoral loose = thigh/knee pain.** Worse with rotation, better with rest
- Early pain after THA: think **infection, poor fixation, or referred pain**
- Diagnosis accepted: progressive lucency/migration + weight-bearing pain relieved by rest
- Asymptomatic but **progressive bone loss**: revise anyway (later = harder)
- RSA (roentgen stereophotogrammetric analysis) very sensitive but not routine

> [!samajh] Exam point
> Loosening ka matlab radiolucency + **progression**. Ek baar ki non-progressive line (zone 1) sirf tension nahi. Aur hamesha **infection exclude karo**.

## 5. Osteolysis [[p.282-285]]

- Final pathway: **host response to particles** (poly, metal, cement). Seen with loose and well-fixed implants, also MoM and ceramic
- **Particle size < 1 micron, > 1 billion/gram** tissue (Maloney). **Macrophages** release TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6, RANKL, M-CSF: osteoclasts up, osteoblasts down (Fig 3.141)
- **Effective joint space (Schmalzried):** debris reaches any bone touched by joint fluid. Explains distal femoral lysis behind a **non-circumferential porous coat**, and dome lysis behind screw holes (also in solid shells)
- **Patterns:** limited porous coat = distal cortical lesions; extensive coat = proximal (GT, LT) lesions (less loosening). Cup: thin poly, incongruent or poorly locked liners = more lysis; **peripheral and retroacetabular lesions**
- Often **asymptomatic till catastrophic** (migration, fracture). Follow-up X-rays every **6-12 months**. X-ray under-estimates (3-D problem). Differentiate from stress shielding

### Management
:::flow
[teal] Loose implant, or large lesion, or progressing | Revise [[p.283]]
v
[amber] Stable stem, bone loss | Graft defect, keep stem (3 series: no loosening, graft incorporated at 3-5 yrs)
v
Stable cementless cup | Options: **liner + head exchange alone** ;; **+ graft lesions (through holes/periphery)** ;; full cup revision
:::

- Narkbunnam score (zones I-III, total 10): **>= 5 = cup loosening likely** (Table 3.5)
- Hamilton: re-revision ~10% (instability, loosening). NZ registry: head/liner exchange **75% survival at 10 yrs**; about half of re-revisions for instability
- Requirements for liner exchange + graft: shell well fixed, locking mechanism intact, replacement liner thickness/design available, lesion reachable. Maloney: all 35 stable
- Revise fully if: **broken locking mechanism, liner wear-through, malpositioned cup** (Restrepo: retained shell loosening 10%)
- **Cement a liner into the shell** when lock is bad: 2-mm mantle, score back of liner; pods give best strength (Springer: no dissociation)

## 6. Adverse local tissue reaction (ALTR) [[p.285-286]]

- Three settings: **metal-on-metal bearings, modular neck stems, modular head-stem taper** (often overlap)
- Think of it in **any persistently painful THA**
- Work-up: history/exam; **ESR/CRP/D-dimer** (can be raised by ALTR alone); **serum cobalt and chromium**; **MARS MRI or ultrasound**; aspiration (look at fluid, **manual cell count** because automated counts falsely positive; **alpha-defensin false positive 31%**)

| Setting | Co / Cr / ratio cut-offs (µg/L) |
|---|---|
| Metal-on-poly taper | Co 1 (sens 95 / spec 94), Cr 2 (83/72), ratio 1 |
| MoM ASR | Co 3.2, Cr 2.4, ratio 1.4 |
| Modular neck | Co 2.8, Cr 3.8 |

(approximate; check Table 3.6 [[p.286]])

### Revision for ALTR (Technique 3.9)
- Fluid cultures + >= 3 tissue samples + frozen section
- Debride **pseudotumor and necrosis**, avoid neurovascular injury and instability
- Remove head, look at **taper corrosion**; retain stem if taper clean (Bovie scratch pad or wet sponges); ETO if modular neck stem well fixed
- Fixed neck + modular head: **ceramic head + titanium sleeve**, poly liner
- Metal liner in modular shell: keep shell if well positioned, poly liner. **Monoblock MoM cup: consider dual mobility** if well fixed and well positioned
- Keep capsule; abductor damage: repair or **gluteus maximus transfer (Technique 3.8)**
- Results **poor**: high infection, instability, reoperation. Refer to a centre

> [!flag] ALTR me CRP/ESR high ho sakta hai bina infection ke. Isliye culture aur frozen section hamesha.
