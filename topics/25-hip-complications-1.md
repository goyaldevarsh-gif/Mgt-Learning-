## 1. Mortality and hematoma [[p.253-254]]

- Mortality (2014 meta-analysis): **30-day 0.3%**, **90-day 0.65%**. Risk: old, male, CVS disease
- **Bleeding sources:** obturator branches (ligamentum teres, TAL, inferior osteophytes); **first perforator of profunda** (under gluteus maximus insertion); femoral branches (anterior capsule); gluteal vessels. **Iliac vessels** at risk with medial wall penetration or removal of a protruded cup
- Late bleed (~1 wk): **false aneurysm** or **iliopsoas impingement** from cup (arteriogram, embolisation, cup revision)
- Most settle conservatively. **Surgery if:** wound dehiscence/marginal necrosis, nerve palsy, infected hematoma. Culture, closed suction drain

## 2. Heterotopic ossification [[p.254]]

| Brooker grade | Description |
|---|---|
| I | Islands of bone in soft tissue |
| II | Spurs, **>= 1 cm** gap between surfaces |
| III | Spurs, **< 1 cm** gap |
| IV | Ankylosis |

- Visible by 3-4 weeks; matures in **1-2 yrs**
- **Risks:** prior HO, hypertrophic OA, **AS, DISH, Paget**, male, African-American; **anterior/anterolateral > posterior/transtrochanteric** approach
- Symptomatic mainly grade III/IV
- **Prophylaxis only in high risk:** **radiation 500 cGy** (within 8 h pre-op or 72 h post-op; shield ingrowth surfaces) or **NSAIDs** (6 wks; 7 days also worked; **COX-2 preferred**, same efficacy)
- Excision rarely needed; wait for **decreased Tc bone scan activity** (mature bone); then prophylaxis again

## 3. Thromboembolism [[p.254-255]]

- Without prophylaxis: DVT **50%**, fatal PE **2%** (old data). Recent PE **0.21%**
- **80-90% of clots in operated limb**; DVT peak 4-17 days; many events post-discharge
- ACCP options: LMWH, fondaparinux, apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban, low-dose UFH, warfarin, **aspirin**, IPC. **Minimum 10-14 days, up to 35 days**
- High bleeding risk: mechanical only. Prior VTE: **drug + mechanical**
- Author practice: low-risk = **aspirin + compression up to 5 wks**; high-risk = **LMWH or apixaban up to 5 wks**

## 5. Vascular injury [[p.256-257]]

- Rare: **0.04% primary, 0.2% revision**; mortality **7-9%**, amputation **15%**, disability 17%
- Risks: revision, intrapelvic migration; causes: screws, cement, cables, cages, threaded cups, allografts, sharp retractors
- **Quadrant system (Wasielewski):** line from ASIS through acetabular centre + perpendicular line.
  - **Anterosuperior = external iliac vessels**
  - **Anteroinferior = obturator vessels + nerve**
  - **Posterosuperior = best bone, longest screws** (sciatic notch: superior gluteal vessels, sciatic nerve; palpate drill tip)
  - Posteroinferior = short screws. **Prefer posterior quadrants**
- Bleeding not controlled: retroperitoneal exposure, clamp iliac, vascular surgeon, angiography/embolisation
- Persistent incision bleeding or pulsatile mass = **false aneurysm** (also after infection, threaded cup migration, pointed retractors)
- **Before removing a protruded cup:** CT/angiography, prep abdomen, vascular surgeon
- Pelvic positioners on **pubic symphysis or iliac spines**, never femoral triangle

## 6. Limb-length discrepancy [[p.257-259]]

- **Lengthening causes:** insufficient neck cut, long neck, **cup placed inferior**, high femoral cortical index (FCI) [[p.257]]
- 30% perceive LLD, only 36% of them confirmed on X-ray. Perception also depends on **pelvic obliquity, knee flexion/recurvatum, tibial height**
- Contracture: flexion + adduction = apparent **shortening**; abduction = apparent lengthening
- Clinical leg length matches radiograph within 1 cm in only **50%** (Edeen)
- **Offset vs length:** if offset is reduced, you may overlengthen to get soft-tissue tension, so restore offset
- **Shuck test:** traction in extension gives **2-4 mm** distraction. Not reliable alone
- Methods: pin at infracotyloid groove vs trochanter mark (Ranawat: mean error 1.9 mm); **templating + intra-op measurement** best (Woolson: 2.5% > 6 mm; Knight: within 5 mm in 92%)
- Navigation improves accuracy but **not clinical outcome**
- Priorities: **pain relief, stability, mobility, equal leg length (in that order)**. A longer but stable hip beats a short unstable one
- < 1 cm well tolerated; perception fades. Unacceptable LLD: look for **maloriented cup** (Parvizi: most needed cup revision; average 4 cm to 1 cm)

## 7. Dislocation [[p.259-263]]

### Basics
- Historical rate ~**3%**; revision **9.04%** (4656 revisions)
- **Patient factors:** trochanteric nonunion, weak abductors, high pre-op ROM, prior hip surgery, female, old, high ASA, prior hip fracture, cervical myelopathy, spinopelvic imbalance, Parkinson, dementia, depression, osteonecrosis, inflammatory arthritis
- **Surgeon factors:** malposition, impingement, soft tissue tension, small head
- **Revision risks:** age, small head, previous dislocation, >= 2 revisions, non-elevated liner
- **Posterolateral approach:** cup tends to be **retroverted**. **Soft-tissue repair cut rate from 4.46% to 0.4%** (meta-analysis). DAA vs posterior: no difference in level I studies (Wang)
- Most within **3 months**; late ones recur (Von Knoch: **55% recurrent**, 61% of those needed surgery)

### Cup position
- **Lewinnek safe zone:** inclination 40 ± 10, anteversion 15 ± 10. Many dislocations occur within it
- Pelvic tilt: lateral position pelvis is flexed ~**35 deg**; forceful femur retraction tilts the patient forward = retroversion. Women (broad hips) get cup too horizontal; men (broad shoulders) the reverse

| Spinopelvic type | Inclination | Anteversion | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 35-45 | 15-25 | 25-45 |
| **Stiff** | 45-50 (50 only elderly) | 20-25 | 35-45 |
| Kyphotic | 35-40 | 15-20 | 25-35 |
| Hypermobile | 35-40 | 12-20 | 25-35 |

(Ike, Dorr; Table 3.1 [[p.260]])

![Fig 3.112: CT measurement of cup anteversion (20 deg retroversion)](figs/h3_112.jpg)

| Malposition | Dislocates |
|---|---|
| Cup too anteverted | **Anterior**: extension, adduction, ER |
| Cup retroverted | **Posterior**: flexion, adduction, IR |
| Cup too vertical (e.g. 65 deg) | Superior with adduction |
| Cup too horizontal | Impingement early flexion, posterior dislocation |
| Stem > 15 deg anteverted | Anterior |
| Stem retroverted | Posterior (flexion + IR) |

- Femoral version check: knee at 90 deg, **neck perpendicular to tibia = neutral**; stem aim **5-15 deg** anteversion. **Combined anteversion (Dorr/Amuwa): goal 35, range 25-50**
- **Impingement:** bone/cement beyond cup rim acts as fulcrum; retained anterior osteophytes (CT), enlarged trochanter, low offset
- **Head:neck ratio:** skirted heads lower it. Elevated liner can help or hurt. **Dual mobility 0.9% primary, 1.3% revision** (De Martino)
- **Trochanteric nonunion:** dislocation **17.6% vs 2.8%** (Woo and Morrey)

### Recurrent dislocation: Wera algorithm [[p.263]]

| Type | Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| I | Acetabular malposition | Revise cup |
| II | Femoral malposition | Revise stem |
| III | Abductor deficiency | **Constrained or dual mobility** |
| IV | Impingement | Remove source, restore offset, larger head |
| V | Late wear | Head + liner exchange, larger head |
| VI | Unresolved | Constrained / dual mobility |

- Re-dislocation 14.6%, highest in type III
- No cause found: **distal advancement of greater trochanter** (Kaplan: 17 of 21 had no more dislocations)
- **Constrained liners:** average recurrent dislocation **10%**; Callaghan: tripolar 6-7% failure. **Guyen failure types I-V** (bone-implant, liner-shell, locking ring, inner bearing, infection). **Last resort**. Excess impingement levers cup out
- Non-compliant, alcoholic, debilitated, multiple failures: **resection arthroplasty** may be best

> [!samajh] Dislocation ka quick approach
> Direction dekho: **anterior = cup/stem zyada anteverted. Posterior = retroverted ya soft tissue weak.** Fir cause type (Wera) se treatment decide hota hai. Spine stiff ho to anteversion badhao.

:::qa Quadrant system me kaun si quadrants me screw safe?
Posterior quadrants (posterosuperior best). Anterosuperior = external iliac vein, anteroinferior = obturator vessels/nerve [[p.256]].
:::

:::qa Heterotopic ossification prophylaxis kab?
Only high-risk (prior HO, hypertrophic OA, AS, DISH, Paget, male); 500 cGy radiation or NSAIDs [[p.254]].
:::
