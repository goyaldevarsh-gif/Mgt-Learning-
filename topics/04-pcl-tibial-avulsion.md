## Big picture

The PCL attaches to the **back of the tibia** in the posterior fossa. A blow to the front of the flexed knee (dashboard injury, fall on a flexed knee) pushes the tibia backward and the PCL can pull a **bone fragment** off its insertion.

> [!samajh] Bike se girte hue ya car dashboard se ghutna takrata hai, shin peeche dhakel jaati hai. Kabhi ligament beech se phat'ta hai, kabhi tibia ka tukda ligament ke saath nikal aata hai. Avulsion fixable hai, mid-substance tear mushkil hota hai.

## Exam {revise}

- Haemarthrosis (less obvious if capsule torn), pain behind the knee.
- **Posterior sag sign**: knees at 90 degrees, tibia sags backward.
- **Posterior drawer** at 90 degrees: the key test. Grade by step-off between tibial plateau and femoral condyles: 1 (0 to 5 mm), 2 (5 to 10 mm), 3 (more than 10 mm).
- Quadriceps active test, reverse pivot shift.
- Check posterolateral corner (dial test), popliteal vessels and peroneal nerve.

> [!flag] Dashboard injury plus a cold or pulseless foot: think popliteal artery.

## Imaging

- Lateral X-ray: fragment at the posterior tibia, size and displacement.
- CT for fragment size and comminution.
- MRI for associated ligament and meniscal injuries.
- Stress radiographs can quantify posterior translation.

> [!verify] The displacement cut-off (often 2 mm) that decides operative treatment; confirm in your book.

## Treatment

### Non-operative (undisplaced)
- Long-leg brace or cast in **extension** with a posterior support so the tibia is held forward.
- Weeks of protection (commonly 4 to 6), then quadriceps-focused rehab. Avoid hamstring strengthening early because the hamstrings pull the tibia back.

### Operative (displaced)
- **Posterior approach** (Burks and Schaffer, or modified), prone position: reduce the fragment and fix with cannulated screw, or suture/anchors for small fragments.
- Arthroscopic fixation is an option in experienced hands.
- Neurovascular structures are close behind the knee, so protect them.

## Rehab

1. Early: brace in extension, prone passive flexion, no active hamstring work.
2. Later: closed-chain quadriceps work, gait training.
3. Sport return: months.

## Complications

- Posterior laxity from a stretched ligament even if the bone heals.
- Nonunion, stiffness.
- Neurovascular injury during a posterior approach.

## Viva {viva}

:::qa What is the posterior sag sign?
With the knees at 90 degrees, the tibia sags backward relative to the femur.
:::

:::qa How do you grade posterior drawer?
By tibial step-off relative to the femoral condyles: 1 (0 to 5 mm), 2 (5 to 10 mm), 3 (over 10 mm).
:::

:::qa Why avoid hamstring work early?
Hamstrings pull the tibia posteriorly, loading the healing PCL.
:::
