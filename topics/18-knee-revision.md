## 1. Why primary TKAs fail aseptically

- 10-yr survivorship ~**95%**; today **infection and instability** lead; osteolysis/loosening less common [[p.470]]
- **Tibial loosening > femoral.** Linked to malalignment, laxity, activity, poly wear, excess constraint
- **Cemented loosening:** complete radiolucent line **>= 2 mm** at bone-cement interface (Fig 7.85). Incomplete < 2 mm lines are common and benign
- A line under a metal-backed tibia can disappear with **4 deg** of flexion: use comparable views [[p.471]]

## 2. Instability: choose the least constraint

:::flow
[teal] Instability | Use the **lowest level of constraint that works** [[p.471]]
v
AP (flexion space) | Convert to **PS** ;; Varus-valgus (extension space) | **CCK** ;; Global | **Linked/hinge** only if soft tissue cannot be rebuilt
:::

![Fig 7.87: Instability from poly wear or ligament loss may be an indication for revision](figs/f7_87.jpg)

:::flow

v
[amber] V-Y turndown (Scott-Siliski) ;; Tibial tubercle osteotomy | 8-10 cm, can advance for patella baja

## 4. Removing implants

- **Femur first** (clears space for tibia)
- Aim osteotome at **prosthesis-cement interface**, not bone; **slap hammer axial force only** (Fig 7.92) so a condyle does not break
- Tibia: saw through all-poly stem; long cemented stem: long TTO or diamond saw
- Keep a well-bonded, unworn patellar button

![Fig 7.92: Slap hammer delivers longitudinal force to extract the femoral component](figs/f7_92.jpg)

> [!samajh] Implant se ladho, bone se nahi.

## 5. Principles of reconstruction

> [!tip] **9 principles [[p.474]]:** joint line at anatomic level; treat defects, preserve bone (cones, augments); stability (soft tissue or constraint); limb alignment; solid stems; rigid fixation; PF mechanics; modular system; fill both gaps.

- Revision: usually **PS** (PCL scarred or incompetent). **Tibia first**
- **Joint line:** ~**1 fingerbreadth above the proximal fibular head tip** and ~1 fingerbreadth below the inferior patellar pole; from epicondylar axis **23 mm lateral / 28 mm medial** [[p.475]]

![Fig 7.96: Joint line in revision measured from fibular head or epicondylar axis](figs/f7_96.jpg)

| Fix | Fills which gap |
|---|---|
| Thicker poly / tibial augment | **Both** |
| Distal femoral augment | **Extension only** |
| Larger femoral AP size / posterior augment | **Flexion only** |

> [!flag] Dono gaps loose = aksar femur chhota aur upar. **Bada AP femur + augments** use karo, poly mota karke joint line mat uthao.

- Defects < 5 mm cement; contained = graft; non-contained = wedge/block; severe = **cones / sleeves**
- Cement: stagger tibia and femur by **5-7 min** when bone is tenuous; **antibiotic cement** routine

![Fig 7.94: Modular revision system with augments, stems and levels of constraint](figs/f7_94.jpg)

Extension gap only [[p.475]].

## 6. Results

- Worse than primary: 5-yr good/excellent only **46-74%** [[p.476]]
- Extensor mechanism problems and infection more common; re-revisions do worse
- Pain and function **worsened in year 2** (NAKARSG), so follow beyond 1 year
- Poly exchange **< 3 yrs** after primary: ~**4 x** re-revision risk vs > 4 yrs
- Cones/sleeves for AORI IIb and III defects: good mid-term results
