## 1. Periprosthetic femoral fracture

### Intra-operative [[p.263-265]]
- Mayo registry: **1.7% primary, 12% revision**. Cementless > cemented: **3.0% primary, 19% revision** (Abdel). Risks: female, elderly, uncemented stem, RA, osteoporosis, prior hardware
- When: dislocation maneuver (release more capsule, remove osteophytes), broaching/insertion. Broaches **crush cancellous bone, not diaphyseal cortex**: use reamer first if templating shows cortical contact
- Expose fracture to its distal end **with the component in** (gap closes when the implant is out). Remove stem, **cerclage**; trial broach one size smaller to avoid collapse; one cable **distal** to fracture; recommended in all cases (no objective way to call it stable)

| Vancouver (intra-op) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Proximal metaphysis |
| B | Proximal diaphysis, long stem can bypass |
| C | Beyond the longest revision stem |
| Subtypes | 1 perforation, 2 undisplaced, 3 displaced |

### Post-operative [[p.264-267]]
- Mostly **low-energy trauma** (< 10% high-energy). Risk: HO limiting motion, cortical defects, loosening, osteolysis

| Vancouver (post-op) | Location / stem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **A** (AG/AL) | Greater/lesser trochanter | Mostly conservative; fix displaced GT; lesser only if medial cortex + unstable stem |
| **B1** | At/below stem tip, **stem stable** | **ORIF**, stem left. Rigid: **locking plate**, proximal + distal screws, strut graft. Wires/bands/lone screws fail |
| **B2** | Stem **loose** | **Revise to long cementless stem** (extensively coated, fluted tapered, modular) + cerclage/strut/graft |
| **B3** | Stem loose + **bone stock poor** | Allograft-prosthesis composite, proximal femoral replacement, or **tapered fluted** stem |
| **C** | Well below stem | ORIF only, avoid stress riser at stem tip |
| **D** (Duncan-Haddad) | Between hip and knee arthroplasty | Plate if both stable, else revise one/both |

- Springer (118 fractures): better with **extensively porous-coated** stems
- Table 3.2 is Duncan and Masri's Vancouver [[p.267]]

### Acetabular fracture [[p.265-266]]
- Intra-op: **0.4%** (7121 primaries; all cementless, mostly one monoblock elliptical design). Most stable, retain cup (+ screws)
- Hickerson algorithm (Fig 3.125): by wall/column and cup stability. Unstable cup: **cup-cage / antiprotrusio cage**

## 2. Trochanteric nonunion [[p.267-269]]

- Risks: small fragment, poor bone, inadequate fixation, excess abductor tension, prior radiation, noncompliance
- Mechanism (Charnley): **AP shear in flexion** loading (chair, stairs) comes first, then fatigue failure of fixation and proximal migration
- Incidence **3-8% primary**, **9-13% revision**. Union better with **trochanteric slide, circumferential (extramedullary) cables, good apposition** (McCarthy)
- **Fibrous union without migration:** good function. **Migration > 2 cm** impairs abductors (Amstutz). Dislocation 17.6% vs 2.8%
- Painful wires: injection test; removal gives relief in < 50%. Braided-cable fragmentation = metal debris, accelerated wear, so remove cables if fretting/nonunion
- Repair only if painful pseudarthrosis or significant abductor weakness. **Wire alone fails.** Hodgkinson crossover wire + spring: 81%; claw plate + wires 51/72. Abduct only **10-15 deg**; bone graft; delay weight bearing; brace/spica
- Chin and Brick: advance abductors by releasing iliac origin
- **Abductor avulsion** (direct lateral): late repair mixed; gluteus maximus flap or Achilles allograft in small series

### Technique 3.8: gluteus maximus + TFL transfer (primary abductor deficiency) [[p.269-270]]
- Posterior approach; split gluteus maximus middle third; raise flap; trough 4 cm in lateral GT; flap sutured with No.5 nonabsorbable at neutral abduction; fascia lata flap over both
- **Post-op:** 6 wks touch-down WB, standing abduction + full WB at 6 wks, side-lying at 8 wks, strengthening at 3 mo, cane for a year

## 3. Periprosthetic joint infection (PJI)

### Epidemiology and risk [[p.270-271]]
- Current infection rate **1-2%** (Charnley 6.8%; Wilson 11%)
- Risk calculator (Tan): **revision THA 50 pts vs primary 18**; prior procedures 60-100; drug abuse 62; HIV 49; coagulopathy 38; renal 35; CHF/psychosis 31; DM 19; smoking 10
- 4 routes: **direct contamination, local spread from superficial wound, haematogenous, reactivation** of latent infection
- Organisms: **S. aureus and coagulase-negative staph** (US 35% / 31%); gram-negatives mostly haematogenous (urinary). Sinus = mixed
- Prophylaxis: **cefazolin or cefuroxime**; vancomycin for MRSA carriers; clindamycin for allergy [[p.271]]

### Classification
| Tsukayama | Trampuz/Zimmerli |
|---|---|
| 1 Early post-op (< 1 month) | Early **< 3 months** |
| 2 Late chronic (> 1 mo, insidious) | Delayed **3-24 months** |
| 3 Acute haematogenous (> 1 mo, acute, distant source) | Late **> 24 months** |
| 4 Positive intra-op cultures | |

### Diagnosis [[p.272-274]]
- Clues: pain from a well-functioning THA, wound drainage history, repeated erythema, long antibiotics. X-ray usually normal or looks like loosening
- Labs: **ESR > 30, CRP > 10 mg/L**, D-dimer (850 ng/mL). WBC not sensitive
- **Aspiration** if any one is raised or suspicion high; **stop antibiotics >= 2 weeks first**; fluoroscopy/US; 18-gauge needle from anterior (lateral to femoral artery) and **touch the neck of the component**; send culture + cell count/diff; leukocyte esterase and alpha-defensin
- Do **not** aspirate if wound infection thought to be superficial only

### Treatment decision [[p.274-277]]

:::flow
[teal] Early post-op or acute haematogenous | Stable implants, good soft tissue, sensitive organism
v
DAIR | Debride, irrigate, **exchange liner + head**, 3+ cultures, **IV antibiotic ~6 wks**, success 20-100% (KLIC / CRIME80 scores)
v
[red] Late chronic, or loose implant | **Remove all implants and cement**, debride, antibiotic spacer
v
Two-stage | Reimplant at ~3 months when ESR/CRP improving, aspirate negative
:::

- **Superficial infection:** debride, do not aspirate. Open to fascia; if in doubt aspirate joint
- **Chronic:** no retention (poor results). >= 3 cultures; **Gram stain useless**; avoid narrow skin bridges
- **Haematogenous:** broad-spectrum IV; debridement within 5 days - 3 months window debated; Hansen: immediate exchange, 70% kept implant
- **Dental prophylaxis (AAOS/ADA 2012):** might stop routine antibiotics; no strong evidence either way
- **One-stage:** reasonable if effective antibiotics, known organism, **no sinus**, no sepsis. **Two-stage:** septic/compromised patient, unknown organism, virulent/resistant bug, sinus, bad soft tissue. 168 pts: **87.5% infection-free at 7 yrs**
- Two-stage 90-day mortality **2.6%** (higher than carotid endarterectomy, prostatectomy, kidney transplant)
- **PROSTALAC:** antibiotic cement around a simple stem + all-poly cup; 89% success at 10-15 yrs (Biring); keeps length and mobility; risks dislocation/fracture of spacer
- Reimplant: sciatic nerve in scar, capsulectomy + iliopsoas/max release, superior defect, prophylactic cerclage, 3-site cultures, frozen section (> 10 PMN/HPF = re-debride)
- **Failed two-stage:** repeat two-stage only 36-40% success; **resection (Girdlestone)** resolves infection but function poor; **hip disarticulation** only to save life
