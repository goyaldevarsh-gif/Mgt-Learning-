## 1. Forces on the hip

- Body weight acts on a long lever arm, **abductors on a short one**. Ratio ~**2.5:1** normally, so abductors need ~**2.5 x BW** to hold the pelvis level in single-leg stance [[p.179-180]]
- Load on femoral head in stance ~**3 x BW** (same in straight-leg raise)
- **Arthritic hip:** lever-arm ratio may be **4:1**. Bringing it toward **1:1** reduces total load ~**30%** (theory)
- Charnley idea: deepen the acetabulum (shortens body-weight arm) and move the greater trochanter laterally (lengthens abductor arm)
- Today most THAs are done **without trochanteric osteotomy**, so only **offset** changes the abductor arm; medialisation has given way to preserving subchondral bone [[p.180]]

![Fig 3.1: Lever arms acting on the hip joint](figs/h3_1.jpg)

| Situation | Joint force | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Gait, calculated | 3.5-5 x BW (up to 6 x single-limb) | 180 |
| Single-limb stance, measured in vivo | 2.6-3 x BW | 180 |
| Lifting, running, jumping | up to **10 x BW** | 180 |
| Out-of-plane force on stem | 0.6-0.9 x BW | 180 |

- Forces act in coronal **and** sagittal plane (centre of gravity is behind the joint axis): stem bends **posteriorly**, worst when hip flexed (chair, stairs, lifting). Together = **torsion** of stem (Fig 3.2)
- So cementless stems must be **rotationally stable at once**; cemented stems need a shape that locks in the mantle
- **Hip centre:** anatomic position gives lowest joint reaction force. Superior displacement alone raises periacetabular stress little (useful in dysplasia/revision), but non-anatomic centres show more radiolucency and migration [[p.180]]

> [!samajh] Offset ka matlab
> Abductor arm lamba = abductor ko kam force lagani padti hai = joint load kam + limp kam + impingement kam. Isliye THA mein **offset restore karna** zaroori hai.

:::qa Abductor force single-leg stance mein kitni chahiye?
~2.5 x body weight (lever-arm ratio ~2.5:1) [[p.180]].
:::

## 2. Stress transfer to bone

### Dorr femur types [[p.181]]

![Fig 3.3: Dorr types A, B, C](figs/h3_3.jpg)

| Type | Look | Who | Fixation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Thick cortices, narrow distal canal, **champagne flute** | Men, younger | Good for cemented or cementless |
| **B** | Medial and posterior cortical loss, wider canal, shape kept | - | Implant fixation not a problem |
| **C** | Much cortical loss, very wide, **stovepipe** | Older postmenopausal women | Less favourable for cementless |

### Stress shielding

- Stem **bending stiffness ∝ diameter⁴**. Bigger stem = much stiffer = more shielding [[p.181]]
- Moderate/severe proximal resorption: almost all in stems **>= 13.5 mm**
- Cementless stems lose more proximal bone than cemented; **CoCr ~2 x** the loss of titanium. Loss progresses for >= 5 years, **not seen in resurfacing** [[p.181-182]]
- Tapered titanium stems: proximal atrophy only **6%** (748 hips) [[p.182]]
- Collar: loads proximal medial neck, but on cementless stems it may stop full seating
- Proximal wedging of a collarless stem gives **hoop stress**, so intra-op fracture; **prophylactic cerclage** raises energy to failure
- Patients with low BMD pre-op lose most bone

![Fig 3.4: Stress shielding. Extensively porous-coated stem, 2 yrs later proximal density has dropped](figs/h3_4.jpg)

> [!samajh] Clinical meaning
> Engh: stress shielding gave lower walking scores but **no more loosening/revision**. Issue yeh hai ki **revision mein bone stock kam** milta hai.

### Acetabular side [[p.182-183]]

- Subchondral bone removal + **thin poly cup** = highest cement/trabecular stress
- **Poly >= 5 mm** or metal backing lowers stress. Cemented metal-backed cups: **no sustained benefit**, some series worse
- Cementless cup: metal must contact subchondral bone widely. **Undersized** shell: stress at pole, gaps at rim. **Oversized**: peripheral stress, **rim fracture** risk (Fig 3.5)

## 3. Femoral component: what to restore

:::flow
[teal] Centre of rotation | Restore 3 things [[p.183-184]]
v
Vertical height | neck length ;; Offset | stem design ;; Version | 10-15 deg anteversion
:::

![Fig 3.6: Features of the femoral component](figs/h3_6.jpg)

- **Morse taper** ~3 deg per side; common **12/14**. Manufacturers differ: **do not mix heads and trunnions** (toggling, corrosion) [[p.184]]
- Neck length 25-50 mm; adjustments of 8-12 mm. Skirted head needed for long necks only with heads <= 32 mm
- **High-offset** stems: neck-stem angle ~**127 deg** or neck attached more medially. Medial attachment = offset up, **height unchanged** (Fig 3.9) [[p.184]]
- **Dual modular necks:** tribocorrosion, recalls, use declining

![Fig 3.9: Increasing offset by reducing neck-stem angle (A) or moving the neck medially (B)](figs/h3_9.jpg)

> [!samajh] Offset vs length
> Sirf lamba neck lagane se offset bhi badhta hai par **limb bhi lamba ho jaata hai**. Medial neck attachment se **limb length nahi badalti**.

## 4. Head size and stability

- Bigger head = bigger **head-neck ratio** = more motion before neck hits rim, and larger **jump distance** (~half the head diameter) [[p.185]]
- Lombardi: **36 mm or larger** heads, dislocation only **0.05%**
- Barrack: 28 to 32 mm gives **+8 deg flexion**
- Best configuration: **trapezoidal neck + larger head, no skirt**

## 5. Cemented stems

- **CoCr** stem preferred (higher modulus). Broad medial border; **no sharp edges** [[p.186]]
- Failure starts at **prosthesis-cement interface** (debonding). Matte stems loosened more than identical **polished** ones (Exeter, Charnley)
- **CPT (collarless, polished, tapered)**: taper-slip / force-closed, allows controlled subsidence; **avoid collar** [[p.187-188]]
- Stem fills ~**80%** of canal; cement mantle ~**4 mm proximal, 2 mm distal**; neutral position
- Lengths **120-150 mm**
- Registry: patients **> 75 yrs** do better cemented (fewer periprosthetic fractures) [[p.186]]
- **Precoating** stem with PMMA: higher failure, abandoned

![Fig 3.18: CPT stem (polished, tapered, collarless)](figs/h3_18.jpg)

## 6. Cementless stems

- Needs **immediate stability** + **intimate bone contact** [[p.188]]
- **Titanium** (lower modulus, notch-sensitive: porous coat only proximally) or **CoCr** (beaded). Thigh pain relates to **stem size**, not material (Lavernia)
- **Porous coat** circumferential proximally (stops particle "effective joint space" osteolysis). Pore size **100-400 µm** [[p.188]]
- **Ongrowth:** grit blast 3-8 µm; plasma spray. **HA** 50-155 µm: mixed results, not harmful [[p.188-189]]

### Khanuja stem types [[p.189-191]]

| Type | Design | Prep | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** Single wedge | Flat AP, tapered ML | Broach only | Wedge proximally; good in Dorr B/C |
| **2** Dual wedge | Tapered ML + AP | Ream + broach | Safe in Dorr A |
| **3** Tapered, metaphyseal-diaphyseal | 3A conical, 3B fluted, 3C rectangular | Ream | 3B popular in complex revision |
| **4** Extensively coated | Full-length fixation | Cylindrical reaming | Excellent long term; shielding, thigh pain; hard in Dorr C |
| **5** Modular | Sleeve + stem | Both reamed | DDH, osteotomy; modular-junction concerns |
| **6** Anatomic | Posterior bow | Needs L/R | Declining; anterior cortex point loading |

![Fig 3.21: Taperloc, a type 1 single-wedge stem](figs/h3_21.jpg)

![Fig 3.26: S-ROM modular stem. Sleeve can be rotated relative to stem](figs/h3_26.jpg)

- **Young patients:** extensively coated stems, no loosening at >= 10 yrs (Takenaga); single wedge < 50 yrs, no aseptic revision at 20 yrs; HA-coated **93.6% at 30 yrs** [[p.192]]
- **> 75 yrs:** more periprosthetic fracture revisions with cementless
- **Short stems:** bone-sparing, used with anterior approach; limited data, risk of varus and subsidence [[p.192]]
- **Custom / CAD-CAM:** rare; deformity, tumour, fracture below stem tip

## 7. Acetabular components

- **Cemented cup:** all-poly, grooves, **3 mm PMMA spacers** (avoid bottoming out). Survivorship not much better over time, so trend is cementless. All-poly still ok in **older, low-demand** [[p.193]]
- **Cementless cup:** porous shell, **1-2 mm oversize** press-fit; **screws** give best ingrowth (risk to intrapelvic vessels); shells 40-75 mm; heads 22-40 mm [[p.194]]
- Liner problems: **backside wear**, dissociation. **Poly < 5 mm** = high stress, so small cup needs smaller head
- HA coating on cups: no survivorship gain. Solid shells (no holes): no help for osteolysis

![Fig 3.35: Dual mobility acetabular component](figs/h3_35.jpg)
