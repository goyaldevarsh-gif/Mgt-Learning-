## 2. Osteoarthritis [[p.230-231]]

- Limb shortened, hip **flexed, ER, adducted** (apparent shortening); rarely abduction contracture = apparent lengthening
- **Medialise** if lateral subluxation + medial osteophytes, else cup rests on osteophytes (Fig 3.80)
- Enlarged greater trochanter may need trimming to avoid impingement

## 3. Protrusio acetabuli [[p.233-234]]

- **Primary** (Otto pelvis/arthrokatadysis): young women, bilateral. **Secondary:** Paget, Marfan, RA, AS, osteomalacia, prior fracture, septic arthritis
- Hallmark: head medial to **ilioischial (Kohler) line**

![Fig 3.81: Primary protrusio (Otto pelvis), head medial to Kohler line](figs/h3_81.jpg)

:::flow
[teal] Principles | 1) Anatomic hip centre 2) Use the **intact peripheral rim** 3) Graft medial defects [[p.234]]
v
Expose | **Sciatic nerve is closer**: find and protect. If head locked in, **in-situ neck cut**
v
Ream | **Do not ream medial wall**; ream periphery only so walls converge ("hourglass")
v
Graft + cup | Impact cancellous graft with last reamer reversed; cup **1-2 mm oversize**
v
[amber] Expect lengthening | Low neck cut + **high-offset stem**
:::

- **Sloof** impaction grafting + mesh + cemented cup (RA): **90% survival at 12 yrs**; weight bearing limited 3 months

## 4. Developmental dysplasia [[p.234-241]]

### Crowe classification (AP pelvis)
Migration = vertical distance, **interteardrop line to medial head-neck junction**, as % of vertical diameter of opposite head (or 20% of pelvic height).

| Crowe | Subluxation |
|---|---|
| I | < 50% |
| II | 50-75% |
| III | 75-100% |
| IV | > 100% (high dislocation) |

![Fig 3.84: Dysplasia types: superior defect, false acetabulum, high dislocation](figs/h3_84.jpg)

- Femur: small head, **short narrow anteverted neck**, narrow canal (1.5 cm at 2 cm below LT), anterior bow; trochanter small and posterior
- Soft tissue: shortened psoas, rectus, hamstrings, adductors; **sciatic nerve never reached normal length**
- Plan: lengthening **max 3-4 cm**; **cup <= 40 mm** may be needed; **22 mm head** (poly thickness); keep medial wall; no more than **5 mm cup uncovered** (Charnley/Feagin)

### Cup position
- **True acetabulum preferred.** Loosening **2-3 x higher** outside it. Also lowers joint force, helps abductor function and lengthening [[p.236]]
- Crowe I: easy. II/III: superior defect; if not lateralised, graft often not needed
- **Bulk graft** (femoral head, lag screws 2-3, trabeculae along load): loosening about **15%**; early reports 47%. Graft should sit under host-bone buttress, unloaded lateral part resorbs; no ingrowth from bulk graft
- **High hip centre (Harris):** small porous cup superior/medial; up to ~1 cm acceptable; risks **impingement + instability** (resect anterior column / ischium)
- **Crowe IV:** small cup in true acetabulum, no structural graft; find the **ledge** between false and true, transverse ligament, cotyloid fossa; **anterior wall thin, posterior thick**; screws needed, safe zone narrower (obturator vessels)

![Fig 3.86: Cup in false acetabulum: limb 4 cm short, poor abductors](figs/h3_86.jpg)

![Fig 3.87: Superior defect grafted with femoral head, cup in true acetabulum](figs/h3_87.jpg)

### Femoral side
- Crowe I/II: small cemented or cementless stem; neutral to slight anteversion. Excess anteversion: modular stem
- Crowe III/IV: femur must come down several cm; limits are **hamstrings and rectus** more than abductors, so **femoral shortening osteotomy**
  - **Dunn and Hess:** trochanteric osteotomy + metaphyseal resection, **0.5 cm at a time**, trial reductions
  - **Subtrochanteric shortening** (Sponseller/McBeath): below lesser trochanter, stem fixes both fragments, derotate to **10-15 deg anteversion**, cerclage; Ollivier: **89% 10-yr** survival (Crowe IV)
  - Cemented stem: cement leaks into osteotomy, so **cementless preferred** (fluted modular)
- Trochanter: osteotomise if it impinges on pelvis in abduction (move distal) or posteriorly in ER (move lateral); high-offset stem helps

![Fig 3.91: Subtrochanteric femoral shortening osteotomy, steps A-D](figs/h3_91.jpg)

> [!samajh] Dysplasia ka mantra
> **True acetabulum + chhota cup + 22 mm head + femur shorten karo agar limb neeche nahi aa raha.** Sciatic nerve pe tension: jyada lengthening nahi (3-4 cm limit).

:::qa Crowe IV hip me femur shortening kyun?
Distal translation is limited by hamstrings and rectus, not abductors; shortening reduces head into true acetabulum without nerve stretch or fracture [[p.239]].
:::

## 5. Traumatic and post-traumatic [[p.242-247]]

### Acute displaced neck fracture
- Rogmark RCT: failure **43% fixation vs 6% arthroplasty**; THA dislocation 8%
- THA > hemi in function, pain (Blomfeldt, Hedbeck); Iorio: cemented THA most cost-effective; **fixation most expensive**
- **Choose THA:** independent, ambulant, lucid, active. **Hemi:** frail, institutionalised, cognitively impaired
- Dislocation falls **8% to 2%** with anterolateral instead of posterolateral (Sköldenberg); if posterior: larger head + repair
- **Cemented stem** supported by registry data. THA also for Paget neck fractures, RA, pre-existing painful arthritis

### Failed hip fracture fixation [[p.244-246]]
- Send ESR/CRP (infection), fluoroscopy, extraction set; remove hardware **after dislocating** hip (less fracture)
- Neck nonunion: standard stem, long neck. **Trochanteric nonunion: calcar-replacement stem** (preferred over graft)
- Bypass screw holes by **~2 bone diameters**; cortical **strut graft** over plate site; occlude holes in cement
- After cephalomedullary nail: sclerotic canal, prefer cementless
- **Conversion from intertrochanteric fixation worse** than neck: fractures 17% (Morice), complication 41.9% after nail vs 11.7% after sliding hip screw (Pui)

![Fig 3.95: Trochanteric nonunion, calcar replacement stem bypasses screw holes](figs/h3_95.jpg)

### Acetabular fracture [[p.246-247]]
- Usually ORIF first. **Primary THA** in older osteoporotic with comminution, impaction, or pre-existing arthritis; fixation + THA through one approach (Mears: 79% good/excellent)
- Weaver elderly: reoperation **30% ORIF vs 14% THA**
- Old fracture: **Judet views + CT**, posterior wall defect missed = cup **retroverted** = dislocation. Graft posterior wall, or **cup-cage**
- Meta-analysis (Makridis): acetabular survival only **76% at 10 yrs**; HO, infection, dislocation, nerve injury

### Old osteotomy or deformity [[p.247-248]]
- Prior osteotomy hardware buried, screws break, femur fractures on removal: **stage** if complex
- Metaphyseal valgus: modular or calcar stem. Rotation: smaller cemented stem. **Avoid repeat metaphyseal osteotomy**; do subtrochanteric
- Translation > 50% or big angulation: repeat osteotomy, **cementless fluted stem**
- After **periacetabular osteotomy:** acetabulum often **retroverted**; lower cup anteversion

## 6. Hip arthrodesis to THA [[p.248-250]]

- Indications: severe back pain, ipsilateral knee pain, contralateral hip pain, painful pseudarthrosis. **Fused in bad position:** flexed > 30, adducted > 10, any abduction. **Exclude disc/spine cause first**
- Check: past infection, abductor function (palpate with knee flexed to separate TFL), leg length, metal-subtraction CT
- **Trochanteric osteotomy makes it safer** (posterior trochanter overhangs). Sciatic nerve is close
- Neck cut at base of trochanter, leave bone to cover cup at **45 deg** inclination (Fig 3.102). AIIS as landmark; fluoroscopy
- Weak abductors: **constrained or dual mobility** cup. Percutaneous adductor tenotomy if abduction < 15 deg
- Protect 3 months; flexion rarely reaches 90 deg
- Outcomes: Mayo **33% failure by 10 yrs** (spontaneous ankylosis does better than surgical fusion). Meta-analysis (1104): infection 5.3%, instability 2.6%, loosening 6.2%, nerve 4.7%, **abductor problems 13.1%**

![Fig 3.102: Neck osteotomy in fusion conversion, enough bone left for cup cover](figs/h3_102.jpg)

## 7. Metabolic, haematological, renal

| Condition | THA specifics | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Paget** | Protrusio, varus neck, bowing, dense bone: **burr + fluoroscopy**. Bleeding. Bisphosphonate/calcitonin pre-op. **Cementless durable.** THA = procedure of choice (**86% at 7-10 yrs**); internal fixation and hemi unsatisfactory. **HO common** | 250 |
| **Gaucher** | Wide canal, osteopenia, fractures easy, cement fixation poor; bleeding, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, infection; loosening high | 250-251 |
| **Sickle cell** | Transfuse; avoid hypoxia, acidosis, dehydration; epidural + multimodal. **"Femur within femur"**: canal blocked by dense bone, fluoroscopy, ream over guidewire. **ESR useless.** Complications ~**50%**; cementless 98% 10-yr (Ilyas) | 251 |
| **Chronic renal failure** | Poor healing, infection. Transplant ok (Lieberman); **dialysis 81% poor, 19% infection**; cementless extensively coated better | 251 |
| **Hemophilia** | Centre-based. **Inhibitors = contraindication.** Cemented: 65% cup, 44% stem failure at 8 yrs; cementless better. **HIV** + haemophilia raises infection (10.98% vs 2.28%) | 251-252 |

> [!flag] Sickle cell me ESR kisi kaam ka nahi; hematogenous infection ka risk (functional asplenia) hai. Pain crisis aur infection me farq mushkil hota hai [[p.251]].

## 8. Infectious disorders [[p.252-253]]

- **Old pyogenic arthritis** (fused/pseudarthrosis): solid trabecular fusion = no infection. **MRI 100% sensitive** for active infection (Tang). Check ESR, CRP, aspiration, biopsy, **frozen section**; any positive = **two-stage**
- Use old incisions, excise sinuses completely; skin flap if needed
- Kim: no reactivation in 44 hips (acetabular allograft in 60%); quiet for >= **10 yrs** = no recurrence (170 hips). Poor results due to **anatomy**, not infection
- **Tuberculosis:** hip = 2nd commonest site after spine. Few bacilli, hard to diagnose; TB has **little biofilm**, adheres poorly to implants. **Pre-op biopsy + culture/sensitivity** (resistance). Mostly **single-stage**. Systematic review (226 pts): ATT 2 wks pre-op, **6-18 months post-op**, only 3 reactivations. **Sinus = S. aureus superinfection, so two-stage**. Radical debridement either way [[p.252-253]]

## 9. Tumours and neuromuscular [[p.252-253]]

- **Tumour THA candidates:** metastasis with reasonable life expectancy, low-grade (chondrosarcoma, GCT), PVNS. For primary lesions **cure comes before function**: plan with bone scan, CT, MRI. Resected trochanter/subtrochanter: unstable abductors, so custom or **segmental stem** (suture glutei through holes) or allograft-prosthesis composite (step-cut). Acetabulum: cement + cage/ring or Steinmann pins
- **Neuromuscular (CP, brain/cord injury):** **flexion + adduction contracture** predisposes to **posterior dislocation**, so DAA or anterolateral preferred. Release anterior capsule, psoas, **adductor tenotomy**; cup in extra anteversion; **spica 4-6 wks** if unstable or no muscle control; constrained cup occasionally. CP (Houdak): 7% dislocation, otherwise like OA. Polio: dysplasia on the affected side, overuse OA on the normal side

> [!samajh] Neuro patients
> Spastic hip = adducted + flexed + posterior approach = dislocation. Isliye **anterior approach + extra anteversion + tenotomies**.
