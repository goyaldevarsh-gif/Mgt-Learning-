## Big picture

The **syndesmosis** holds the tibia and fibula together at the ankle mortise. It includes the AITFL, PITFL, interosseous ligament and interosseous membrane.

> [!samajh] Ankle ek mortise hai, talus uske beech fit hota hai. Agar tibia-fibula ke beech ka jod dheela ho jaaye, talus ko jagah milti hai aur ankle unstable ho jaata hai. Isliye isko "high" ankle sprain bolte hain, ligament ankle ke upar hai.

- Mechanism: **external rotation with dorsiflexion**, foot planted (football, skiing).
- Recovery is usually **longer** than a lateral sprain.

## Exam {revise}

- Pain above the joint line, anterolateral tibiofibular area, along the interosseous membrane. Swelling can be mild.
- Pain on push-off and on single-leg hop, rather than inversion.
- **Squeeze test**: compress the tibia and fibula at mid calf, pain at the ankle.
- **External rotation (Kleiger) test**: foot dorsiflexed, rotate externally, pain at the syndesmosis.
- Cotton test (lateral translation of the talus), crossed-leg test, dorsiflexion-compression test.

> [!verify] Which tests have best sensitivity and specificity is argued between books. Name two or three and know how to perform them.

## Imaging

- **Weight-bearing X-rays**: AP, mortise and lateral.
- Typical signs of widened syndesmosis: medial clear space above 4 mm, tibiofibular clear space over 6 mm, tibiofibular overlap under 6 mm on AP (under 1 mm on mortise).
- Always check the **fibula up to the knee** if mechanism suggests Maisonneuve.
- MRI shows ligament injury. Stress views or CT (bilateral) for subtle instability.

> [!verify] The millimetre cut-offs and which view they apply to. Compare with your textbook or radiology reference.

## Stable vs unstable {revise}

| | Stable | Unstable |
|---|---|---|
| X-ray | Normal, even on stress | Diastasis or positive stress |
| Ligaments | AITFL partial | Multiple ligaments, interosseous membrane |
| Treatment | Non-operative | Surgery |

:::flow
[teal] Stable: boot or brace, protected weight-bearing
v
Rehab: range of motion, strengthening, balance
v
[red] Unstable or diastasis
v
Reduce mortise, fix with suture-button or syndesmotic screw
:::

## Treatment

### Non-operative
- Walking boot or brace for about 2 to 6 weeks, then progressive weight-bearing and rehab.
- Sports return often **2 to 3 times longer** than a lateral sprain.

### Operative
- Anatomic reduction of the fibula in the incisura is essential. Malreduction causes poor outcomes.
- Suture-button (flexible) fixation or one or two syndesmotic screws.

> [!verify] Number of cortices, screw size and removal timing for screw fixation. Confirm your book.

## Complications

- Chronic pain, instability, **heterotopic ossification** (synostosis).
- Ankle arthritis if the mortise stays widened.
- Malreduction.

## Viva {viva}

:::qa What is the mechanism?
External rotation with dorsiflexion.
:::

:::qa Name the syndesmosis components.
AITFL, PITFL, interosseous ligament, interosseous membrane.
:::

:::qa Why is the recovery longer?
The syndesmosis bears load and rotation with every step, and heals slowly.
:::
