## Big picture

The most common elbow dislocation. A fall on an outstretched hand with axial load, valgus and supination force: the ulna and radius slip **behind** the humerus.

> [!samajh] Elbow ko ek ring ki tarah samjho: pehle lateral side (LCL) khulti hai, phir aage ki capsule, phir medial side (MCL). Isko **Horii circle** (O'Driscoll's posterolateral rotatory instability) kehte hain. Jitni zyada ring khulti hai, utna zyada instability.

## Simple vs complex {revise}

- **Simple**: ligament and capsule injury only, no fracture.
- **Complex**: with a fracture (radial head, coronoid, olecranon). The terrible triad is radial head + coronoid + LCL.

## Horii circle stages

| Stage | Structures torn | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | LCL (ulnar part) | Posterolateral subluxation |
| 2 | + anterior and posterior capsule | Incomplete dislocation (coronoid perched) |
| 3A | + posterior band of MCL (anterior band intact) | Dislocated, stable to valgus |
| 3B | + the whole MCL | Dislocated, unstable to valgus |
| 3C | Gross, entire distal humerus stripped | Unstable even in a splint |

> [!verify] Stage numbering and who is torn at which stage: confirm in your book.

## OPD / ER steps

1. Look and feel: swollen, olecranon prominent posteriorly.
2. **Neurovascular status before and after reduction** (brachial artery, median and AIN, ulnar nerve).
3. Pre-reduction X-ray (look for fractures).
4. Reduce under sedation and analgesia: correct any side shift, then flex with traction and push the olecranon forward.
5. **Post-reduction check** (this is the key step): concentric reduction on X-ray, then test stability.

## Stability testing {revise}

- Take the elbow through flexion to extension and note the angle where it starts to subluxate.
- Test with the forearm in **pronation** (stabilises when the LCL is injured), **supination** (when the MCL is injured), and **neutral** (if both are injured).
- Look at the lateral X-ray for the **drop sign**: a widened ulnohumeral gap, often from muscle spasm or soft tissue interposition. Stay in a splint, repeat X-ray.

:::flow
[teal] Reduced, concentric, stable through full arc
v
Splint 90 degrees for a few days, then early active motion
v
[amber] Unstable beyond about 30 to 45 degrees of extension / fracture / drop sign persists
v
Surgery: repair ligaments, fix fractures, consider hinged fixator
:::

## Immobilisation and rehab

- Posterior slab at about 90 degrees, **short duration (about 1 to 2 weeks)** because longer immobilisation causes stiffness.
- Early active-assisted motion. Use a hinged brace or an extension block if needed.
- Forearm position during rehab: pronation if lateral injured, supination if medial injured.

> [!verify] Exact days of immobilisation and the extension block angle (commonly 30 degrees): confirm with your book.

## Complications

- Stiffness (most common), heterotopic ossification.
- Recurrent instability, arthritis.
- Neurovascular: ulnar nerve, brachial artery (rare).

## Viva {viva}

:::qa What is the Horii circle?
The progressive order of soft tissue failure from lateral to medial, in a sequence from LCL to anterior capsule to MCL.
:::

:::qa Why is early movement important?
The elbow stiffens quickly. Prolonged immobilisation leads to contracture.
:::

:::qa How do you choose the forearm position?
Pronate for lateral (LCL) injury, supinate for medial (MCL), neutral if both.
:::

:::qa What is the drop sign?
A widened ulnohumeral gap on the lateral view after reduction. It suggests muscle or soft tissue issues or persistent instability.
:::
