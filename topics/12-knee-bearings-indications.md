## 1. Polyethylene

- TKA is **less conforming** than hip, CR even flatter in sagittal plane, so high contact stress [[p.420]]
- **Double dishing** avoids high-stress areas; **min thickness 8 mm** recommended [[p.421]]

| What went wrong | Why | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon-fibre reinforced poly (1980s) | Catastrophic wear, withdrawn | 421 |
| Heat-pressed surface | Subsurface stress zone, delamination (thin poly) | 421 |
| Gamma in air/oxygen | Oxidation, subsurface white band, even on shelf | 421 |

![Fig 7.27: Carbon-fibre reinforced tibial poly: rapid wear and failure](figs/f7_27.jpg)

![Fig 7.29: Subsurface white band in gamma-in-air poly](figs/f7_29.jpg)

- **Crosslinked poly:** big wear drop in simulators, but knee wear differs from hip; limited for PS post-cam. **Vitamin E poly:** in-vitro benefit, in-vivo unknown [[p.422]]
- Retrievals: post wear (cam), posterior edge loading (osteophytes), anterior post wear (box hits in extension) [[p.422]]

> [!samajh] Wear ki 3 jadein
> **Design** (flat vs dished), **material** (oxidation), **mechanics** (malalignment, thin poly, instability). Surgeon sirf teesre ko control karta hai, isliye alignment + balance + thickness.

## 2. Bearings and fixation

- **Metal backing** standard since early 1980s. Data mixed: 5-yr metal-backed 98% vs all-poly 94% (9200 knees); HSS 7-yr 99% vs 97%. Thick all-poly ok in > 80 yrs [[p.423]]
- Alternative femoral surfaces: oxidized zirconium, titanium nitride. Thin hard coatings can peel ("eggshell effect") [[p.423]]
- **Cement** = durable gold standard. Cementless ingrowth less reliable, concentrated around screws/pegs [[p.424]]
- Press-fit condylar cementless **72% vs 94%** cemented at 10 yrs (partly metal-backed patella) [[p.424]]
- **Swedish registry:** cementless revision **1.4 x** cemented [[p.425]]
- **Stems** for revision, bone loss, constrained implants [[p.425]]

![Fig 7.30: Press-fit Triathlon tibial baseplate with 3-D printed porous surface and spiked pegs](figs/f7_30.jpg)

> [!verify] Book mein dekho: Ezzet wear-reduction line (42%) kis material ke liye hai, extract se clear nahi tha [[p.423]]

## 3. Indications for TKA

- Pain from severe arthritis, correlated with X-ray, **after conservative care failed** [[p.425]]
- First rule out: radicular, **ipsilateral hip**, PVD, meniscal, bursitis
- Younger patients only if major disability (systemic arthritis, osteonecrosis collapse)
- Flexion contracture > 20 deg or severe laxity = operate earlier

| Contraindication | Items |
|---|---|
| **Absolute** | Recent/current knee sepsis; remote infection source; extensor mechanism discontinuity; neuromuscular recurvatum; painless functioning arthrodesis |
| **Relative** | Poor medical status, immunodeficiency, severe ipsilateral hip OA (do hip first), vascular disease, psoriasis in field, venous stasis, neuropathic joint, **BMI >= 45**, recurrent UTI, nearby osteomyelitis |

- AAOS: delaying TKA up to **8 months** to optimise risk factors does not worsen outcome [[p.425]]

> [!samajh] Hip + knee dono kharab
> **Hip pehle**, kyunki hip ke baad rehab asaan hota hai (book).

:::qa TKA se pehle pain ke kaun se non-knee source dekhte ho?
Radicular, referred from ipsilateral hip, PVD, meniscal, bursitis [[p.425]].
:::

:::qa 4 absolute contraindications?
Recent/current knee sepsis, remote ongoing infection, extensor mechanism discontinuity, neuromuscular recurvatum (also painless functioning fusion) [[p.425]].
:::

## 4. UKA

- Oxford (> 500 medial meniscal-bearing): **94% at 10 yrs, 91% at 20 yrs** [[p.425]]
- Technique-sensitive: Gioe, community **89% vs TKA 95% at 10 yrs**. Only **6%** of arthroplasty patients have no UKA contraindication [[p.426]]
- Not easy to revise: major defects in 76% in earlier series; modern results approach primary TKA [[p.426]]

:::flow
[teal] UKA contraindications | (book numbers) [[p.426]]

Inflammatory arthritis ;; Flexion contracture >= 15 deg ;; Arc < 90 deg
v
Varus > 10 deg ;; Valgus > 5 deg ;; ACL deficiency ;; Opposite compartment loss
:::

> [!samajh] Shortcut
> **Inflammatory + stiff + deformed + ACL gone + other side bad** = UKA nahi.

## 5. Patellofemoral arthroplasty (PFA)

- **Ideal:** < 65 yrs, isolated disabling PF OA, minimal coronal deformity, localized pain [[p.426]]
- Good in post-traumatic, Outerbridge IV primary OA, PF dysplasia
- **Commonest failure = tibiofemoral arthritis progression** (so selection is everything)
- Abnormal **Q angle: > 15 deg men, > 20 deg women**; PFA does not fix malalignment [[p.427]]
- Contraindications: tibiofemoral OA, inflammatory arthropathy, valgus > 8 / varus > 5 deg, flexion < 120 or FC > 10 deg, patella baja, obesity (BMI > 30 higher revision) [[p.427]]

## 6. Patellar resurfacing

| Question | Book | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior knee pain, 5-yr RCT | **25% unresurfaced vs 5% resurfaced** | 427 |
| PF complications | 4% resurfaced vs 12% unresurfaced (one large study) | 427 |
| Secondary resurfacing | Inferior pain relief, more complications | 427 |
| Can leave unresurfaced | Primary OA, good cartilage, no eburnation, congruent tracking, normal shape, no inflammatory disease | 430 |
| Weight | Lighter patients do well unresurfaced | 430 |

> [!samajh] Practical rule
> **Anatomic trochlea + healthy patella + OA** = skip. **Inflammatory / eburnated / maltracking** = resurface. Secondary resurfacing se bacho.

## 7. Bilateral and outpatient TKA

- Fat embolism after bilateral: **12%** (Dorr). Prevent by **venting IM canal** (fluted rod, bigger entry hole) [[p.431]]
- Avoid in cardiopulmonary disease; higher cardiac/neuro complications > 70 yrs; BMI >= 30 does not add risk
- Outpatient/23-hour TKA needs full team + **TXA + local pain regimen**

:::qa Bilateral TKA ka main specific risk?
Fat embolism (up to 12%, Dorr); cardiopulmonary disease favours unilateral [[p.431]].
:::
