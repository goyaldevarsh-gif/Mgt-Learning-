## Big picture

The **lateral ligament complex** fails under inversion with plantarflexion.

| Ligament | Role | Fails |
|---|---|---|
| **ATFL** | Resists inversion in plantarflexion | First and most often |
| **CFL** | Resists inversion in neutral/dorsiflexion | Second |
| **PTFL** | Rarely injured | Only with dislocation |

> [!samajh] Pair ban'ta hai: ATFL sabse kamzor aur sabse pehle, phir CFL. PTFL bahut mazboot hai, tabhi tootegi jab ankle dislocate ho.

## Is a fracture hiding? Ottawa rules {revise}

Order an X-ray if there is pain in the malleolar or midfoot zone **and**:

- Bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of either malleolus (6 cm), or
- Bone tenderness at the base of the 5th metatarsal or navicular, or
- Unable to bear weight for 4 steps both immediately and in the ER/OPD.

> [!flag] Also think: fibular fracture, talar dome lesion, lateral process of talus, anterior process of calcaneus, syndesmosis (see high ankle sprain), peroneal injury.

## Grading

- **Grade I**: stretch, mild swelling, can walk.
- **Grade II**: partial tear, moderate swelling and bruising, difficulty walking.
- **Grade III**: complete tear, severe swelling, unstable.

## Tests step by step {revise}

1. **Palpation**: ATFL (in front of the lateral malleolus), CFL (below tip), also base of 5th MT, malleoli, syndesmosis.
2. **Anterior drawer**: foot in about 10 to 20 degrees plantarflexion, pull talus forward while stabilising the tibia. Compare with the other side. Tests **ATFL**.
3. **Talar tilt (inversion stress)**: foot neutral, invert the heel. Tests **CFL** (and ATFL).
4. Squeeze and external rotation tests to exclude a high ankle injury.

> [!verify] Typical difference values for a positive drawer (e.g., more than 3 mm vs other side or over 10 mm absolute) and tilt (about 10 degrees difference). Confirm in your book.

## Treatment: early function {revise}

:::flow
[teal] Acute: protect, rest briefly, ice, compress, elevate
v
Weight bear as pain allows; brace or tape 2 to 6 weeks
v
[amber] Balance and proprioception, peroneal strengthening
v
[red] Persistent instability after 3 to 6 months: MRI, consider surgery
:::

- Modern approach: **POLICE** (protect, optimal loading, ice, compression, elevation) replaces long rest.
- Brace or semi-rigid support for Grade II to III. Short boot only for severe pain.
- Start range of motion early. **Proprioception training** reduces recurrence.
- Surgery (Broström type repair) for chronic instability after failed rehab.

## Medicines and supplements

- Oral or **topical NSAIDs** for short courses: pain and swelling relief.
- Paracetamol if NSAIDs are unsuitable.
- Enzymes, collagen or tendon-support brands exist in the market, but evidence for ankle sprains is limited.

> [!verify] Contents and differences of Indian brands (e.g., Tendocare, T-Heal, Macliga) must be taken from the strip or the manufacturer label. Not stored here to avoid wrong composition. Add the exact ingredients once checked.

## Complications

- Chronic lateral ankle instability, recurrent sprains.
- Talar osteochondral lesion, peroneal tendon injury, impingement.
- Missed syndesmosis or fracture.

## Viva {viva}

:::qa Which ligament tears first?
ATFL, in plantarflexion and inversion.
:::

:::qa How do you test the CFL?
Talar tilt (inversion stress) in neutral.
:::

:::qa What is Ottawa ankle rule for?
To decide who needs an X-ray, with high sensitivity.
:::

:::qa Why does early motion work better than casting?
Faster recovery and return to work, with fewer stiffness problems.
:::
