### Periprosthetic fracture principle (user/speaker, Sun 22 Nov, Day 3)
**Point:** fracture not involving the zone of fixation of the implant: no need to revise, fix it. Mostly THA periprosthetic fracture.
**Inputs (ad):** Vancouver (femur): A (AG/AL trochanteric): usually non-op, fix if displaced/abductor compromise. B1 (around/just below a WELL-FIXED stem): ORIF (plate +/- cables, locking plate, strut graft). B2 (loose stem, good bone): revise to long stem bypassing fracture 2 cortical diameters. B3 (loose stem, poor bone stock): revise with allograft-prosthesis composite/ proximal femoral replacement. C (well below stem): ORIF, independent of stem. Key = is the stem stable? (intra-op test, X-ray signs of subsidence/ lucency). Acetabular: stable cup + fracture not involving fixation, fix; unstable cup, revise. Confirm with speaker: exact scheme he used (Vancouver / UCS), plate-length rule.

### Is the stem loose? Algorithm slide (Day 3, slide 49) + intra-op loose stem
**Slide (sp), flowchart: History / Imaging / Surgery. Any "Yes" = LOOSE STEM:**
- History: thigh pain prior to the periprosthetic fracture.
- Imaging, in order: comminution around stem; cemented? (Yes branch goes off the slide edge; No branch continues); fracture intra-op / first few weeks (uncemented); subsidence; periosteal reaction; fracture at fixation level.
- Surgery: intra-op loose stem (final check).
**Inputs (ad):** prior thigh pain = stem micromotion/ loosening/ stress reaction. Subsidence/ periosteal reaction/ lucency = radiographic loosening. Uncemented stem with fracture in first weeks = never osseointegrated (usually intra-op undersizing/fracture). Fracture at the fixation zone = fixation compromised (= Vancouver B2/B3 logic). Intra-op: after exposure, stress the stem (rotation/ push-pull with fracture reduced/ stem exposed): any motion at stem-bone (or stem-cement) interface = loose; cemented stems: look for cement-bone or stem-cement debonding. If loose: revise (Vancouver B2/B3); if stable: ORIF (B1). Loose-stem-in-doubt rule: if unsure, prepare for both (revision stem + plate set) at surgery.
**Confirm with speaker:** what the "Cemented: Yes" branch leads to (slide cut off at right, "St..." probably "Stable"); exact intra-op test he uses.

### Take home: 50% of all B1 fractures are B2 (Day 3, slide 70, full algorithm)
**Slide (sp):** "Take home message: 50% of all B1 fractures are B2 fractures." Full flowchart (completes slide 49): any Yes = Loose stem.
- History: thigh pain prior to fracture. Imaging: comminution around stem; Cemented?
- Cemented = NO (uncemented): fracture intra-op / first few weeks; subsidence; periosteal reaction; fracture at fixation level.
- Cemented = YES: two designs: composite beam (shaped closed) and polished tapered (force closed). Then: cement fragmented/deficient? Yes = Loose stem.
- Surgery: intra-op loose stem. Yes = Loose; No = STABLE STEM (only now treat as B1).
**Inputs (ad):** meaning = never trust a "B1" label on X-ray alone: half are actually loose (B2), so always run this checklist and test the stem intra-op; have a revision stem on the table. Composite beam (e.g. Exeter-type is actually polished taper; composite beam = rough/matte, bonded to cement e.g. Charnley-type, Lubinus?) : confirm examples with speaker. Polished taper is DESIGNED to subside in the cement mantle (force closed): subsidence alone may be normal in polished taper, but fragmented/deficient cement = failure.
**Confirm with speaker:** examples of composite beam vs polished taper; how subsidence is interpreted in polished tapered stems.
