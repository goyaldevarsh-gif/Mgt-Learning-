## Major takeaways
> [!tip] Sabse zaroori 7 points
> 1. **Constraint pre-op decide karo aur next-level implant table par rakho.** Least constraint jo stable knee de.
> 2. **Clinical-to-OR rule:** pliable <10° = routine + osteophytes. Rigid >15° = extended release + reduction osteotomy. Incompetent MCL = attenuated sleeve ko tighten mat karo, VVC/CCK + stems. Severe recurvatum/weak quads = rotating hinge ya VVC with 0° stop.
> 3. **Severe deformity ke 5 X-ray features:** HKA, joint divergence angle + lateral translation, extra-articular (femoral bowing), bone defect depth, osteophyte burden.
> 4. **Varus pre-op:** (a) kitna correctable (rigid/partial/unstable), (b) sagittal (FFD, recurvatum), (c) joint opening.
> 5. **Medial release ladder, step skip nahi:** osteophytes → deep MCL → posteromedial capsule → medial reduction osteotomy → medial epicondylar sliding osteotomy.
> 6. **Valgus:** skyline view zaroori (patellar tracking). EUA se decide: correctible → medial approach, rigid → lateral approach, LEO distal shift (extension tight) ya posterior shift (FFD).
> 7. **Robot = precision tool,** diabetes, anaemia, infection, expectation ya pain phenotype optimise nahi karta.

### The Clinical-to-OR Translation (table slide)
| Clinical exam finding | Soft-tissue strategy | Implant inventory & constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Pliable varus/valgus (<10°) | Routine exposure + osteophyte clearance | Primary unconstrained / PS (standard poly) |
| Rigid contracture (varus/valgus >15°) | Extended releases + reduction osteotomy | PS / plus poly (stemmed baseplate / augments available) |
| Severe lateral thrust / incompetent MCL | Never chase attenuated sleeve; balance to neutral | Varus-valgus constrained (VVC/CCK) + stems |
| Severe recurvatum / weak quads (<=3/5) | Bone under-resection (4-6 mm); zero capsular release | Rotating hinge knee (RHK) or VVC with 0 deg stop |

**Added:**
- Principle: decide constraint BEFORE surgery from the exam, and keep the next-level implant open on the table (a "one step up" backup). Rule: use the least constraint that gives a stable knee.
- Pliable <10°: the deformity is soft-tissue/osteophyte driven, correct with osteophyte clearance +/- minimal release.
- Rigid >15°: structures are fixed-contracted; stepwise release may not suffice. Reduction osteotomy (e.g. medial tibial/condylar reduction for severe varus; lateral for severe valgus) lets you avoid over-release and the need for more constraint. Augments/stems possible.
- Incompetent MCL: do not tighten or advance the attenuated sleeve; balance to neutral and supply the missing stability by the implant (VVC/CCK) with stems for load transfer.
- Recurvatum/weak quads: keep the flexion/extension gaps tight (under-resect distal femur), avoid releases that lose the posterior restraint; hinge (RHK) or VVC with a 0° extension stop to prevent hyperextension. Quad weakness (e.g. polio) means no active control of the knee, so the implant must prevent recurvatum.
- Caution: constraint raises stress at the bone-implant interface (more loosening risk), hence stems.

### 5 Mandatory X-ray Features (severe deformity planning)
**Slide:** (1) Mechanical deformity angle (HKA); (2) Joint divergence angle & lateral translation (lateral laxity); (3) Extra-articular deformity (coronal femoral bowing); (4) Medial bone defect depth; (5) Osteophyte burden.
**Image:** R long-leg film, annotated: 1 severe deformity (34 deg); 2 large lateral divergence angle; 3 lateral subluxation of tibia; 4 significant coronal bowing of femoral shaft; 5 medial bone loss.
**Added:**
- Joint divergence angle (femoral vs tibial joint-line angle) + lateral translation = measure of lateral laxity/ligament incompetence, i.e. whether the ligaments are still competent (links to the constraint table: incompetent sleeve = VVC/CCK).
- Femoral bowing: changes the distal femoral cut (VCA unreliable with IM rod, a bowed canal pushes the rod; consider extramedullary guide/navigation/robot) and may need a stemmed or shorter stem.
- Medial bone defect depth: sizes the need for augment, cement/screw, bone graft or stem (rule of thumb: defect >5 mm usually augment/cement-screw; bigger needs metal augment/stem, to be confirmed with speaker).
- Osteophyte burden: large osteophytes inflate apparent deformity; clearing them may correct part of it (links to the earlier osteophyte-first rule).
- Together these five decide: release extent, need for osteotomy, constraint level, and stem/augment inventory.

### Things to look for in a varus knee (full annotated long-leg film, R)
**Slide (full image, same case as the 5 mandatory features):** 1 severe deformity (34 deg); 2 large lateral divergence angle; 3 lateral subluxation of tibia; 4 significant coronal bowing of femoral shaft; 5 medial bone loss.
**Added (checklist for a varus knee):**
- Magnitude and level: HKA, plus where the deformity sits (femur vs tibia vs intra-articular); MPTA and mLDFA.
- Fixed vs correctable: stress/valgus-stress films or exam. Fixed + >15 deg = extended release/reduction osteotomy planning.
- Lateral laxity: divergence angle + lateral translation (lateral thrust); if lateral structures are attenuated, plan constraint (VVC/CCK).
- Extra-articular bowing (femur, or tibia): affects IM guide use, VCA, stem need.
- Medial tibial bone loss: depth/extent; augment, cement-screw or stem.
- Osteophytes, esp. posteromedial tibia and femur: clear first.
- Soft-tissue: tight medial structures (sMCL, posteromedial corner/semimembranosus), flexion contracture, patellar position.
- Case caution (my observation, confirm with speaker): this film shows lateral subluxation of the tibia, so plan for a lax lateral side rather than a typical tight-medial varus.

### Pre-op evaluation of the varus knee (speaker points)
1. How much of the varus is correctable? Classify: rigid / partial / unstable.
2. Sagittal plane deformity.
3. Joint opening.
**Added:**
- Correctability: apply valgus stress at ~20-30 deg flexion (and in extension) clinically or on stress X-ray. Rigid = no correction (fixed contracture, needs extended release/osteotomy planning). Partial = corrects part-way (medial contracture + osteophytes). Unstable (passively correctable beyond neutral / lateral opening) = lateral laxity; plan constraint.
- Sagittal: flexion contracture (FFD; measure with heel-prop/goniometer), recurvatum, ROM, extensor lag; FFD >15 deg or recurvatum changes the cut plan (distal femoral cut, posterior release, constraint; link to Clinical-to-OR table). Lateral film also for slope and patella height.
- Joint opening: varus stress opening of the lateral side (lateral gapping/thrust) and medial gap on valgus stress; on X-ray the lateral divergence angle (links to mandatory X-ray feature 2). Tells you whether the lateral compartment is lax and how much ligament sleeve is usable.
- Use these three together with the 5 X-ray features to fix: release plan, constraint level, stems/augments on the table.

### Steps of Medial Release (varus knee)
**Slide:** 01 Removal of medial osteophytes from femur & tibia; 02 Release of deep collateral ligament (deep MCL); 03 Release of posteromedial capsule; 04 Medial reduction osteotomy; 05 Medial epicondylar sliding osteotomy.
**Added:**
- Escalation ladder: do one step, re-check gaps (extension and 90 flexion), only then go to the next. Never skip to a later step.
- 01 Osteophytes: tented sMCL/capsule relaxes (the osteophyte-first rule). Often enough for mild-moderate varus.
- 02 Deep MCL (meniscotibial/deep layer): subperiosteal release from the medial tibia, in small steps; affects both flexion and extension.
- 03 Posteromedial capsule (+/- semimembranosus insertion): mainly tight in extension (FFD + varus); release posteromedial corner when extension gap is tight medially.
- 04 Medial reduction osteotomy (resect extra medial tibial plateau/rim so the tibia fits; "Mullaji" type, see Mullaji technique): reduces the need for further sMCL release and removes medial overhang.
- 05 Medial epicondylar sliding osteotomy: for severe fixed varus, detaches the sMCL origin on a bone fragment, slides it distally, fixes with screw/staple. Keeps the sMCL intact instead of releasing it. Reserved for severe/rigid cases.
- Link: this is the answer to "rigid contracture >15 deg: extended releases + reduction osteotomy" (Clinical-to-OR table). If sMCL still too tight after all this, the options are a thicker/lower-constraint insert vs constraint conversion (VVC) per case.

### Three layers on the medial side of the knee (Warren & Marshall 1979)
**Speaker point:** 3 layers on the medial side.
**Added (standard text):**
- Layer I: deep (crural) fascia, invests sartorius; pes anserinus tendons lie just deep to it.
- Layer II: superficial MCL (sMCL), plus posterior oblique ligament (POL) posteriorly, and MPFL proximally-anteriorly. Semimembranosus attachment area also sits at the posterior part. sMCL is the primary valgus restraint.
- Layer III: joint capsule, with the deep MCL (meniscofemoral + meniscotibial ligaments) as a thickening; attaches to the medial meniscus.
- Clinical link: stepwise medial release goes from deep to superficial structure choice: deep MCL (layer III) first, posteromedial capsule, then sMCL (layer II) only if needed; the sMCL is the one to protect (sliding osteotomy keeps it intact).
- Remember: Layers II and III separate at the anterior-to-posterior part; they fuse posteriorly at the posteromedial corner (pay attention when releasing there, near the saphenous nerve infrapatellar branch / saphenous vein in layer I-II plane).

### Skyline (Merchant) view in valgus knee: importance
**Speaker point:** Skyline view matters in a valgus knee.
**Added (standard reasoning):**
- Valgus knee often has a hypoplastic/deficient lateral femoral condyle, which flattens the trochlear groove and lowers lateral condylar height, so the patella tends to subluxate/tilt laterally (high Q-angle + tight lateral retinaculum).
- Skyline shows: patellar tilt and lateral subluxation, trochlear depth/dysplasia, lateral facet wear, lateral condyle hypoplasia.
- Why it matters for TKA: predicts patellar maltracking risk and need for lateral retinacular release; guides femoral component rotation (avoid internal rotation; external rotation helps tracking); and warns of a deficient posterolateral condyle (posterior referencing can under-size/internally rotate femur; use TEA/Whiteside as a check).
- Also helps decide patellar resurfacing and medialising the patellar button and femoral/tibial components.
- Take-home: in valgus, plan the patella (tracking) as carefully as the coronal plane.

### Mullaji classification of valgus knees (title partly hidden by overlay, reads "Mullaji Classification"; confirm)
**Slide:** Type 1 correctible valgus, no associated deformity, MCL intact. Type 2 rigid valgus, no associated deformity, MCL intact. Type 3 valgus + hyperextension deformity, MCL intact. Type 4 valgus + flexion deformity, MCL intact. Type 5 severe valgus with incompetent MCL. Type 6 valgus with extra-articular deformity.
**Added:**
- Logic: ladder of difficulty = correctable (1) -> rigid (2) -> sagittal problems (3 recurvatum, 4 FFD) -> MCL incompetence (5) -> extra-articular (6).
- Type 1: standard TKA, osteophyte clearance +/- limited lateral release; unconstrained/PS.
- Type 2: needs graded lateral release (pie-crusting of ITB/LCL/popliteus per tightness: tight in extension = ITB, tight in flexion = LCL/popliteus), usually still PS.
- Type 3: hyperextension: avoid over-resecting distal femur, keep gaps tight; consider PS with extension stop or more constraint (links to Clinical-to-OR: recurvatum).
- Type 4: FFD: posterior capsule release, remove posterior osteophytes, may need extra distal femoral cut.
- Type 5: incompetent MCL: do not tighten the attenuated sleeve; VVC/CCK (+ stems), or hinge if grossly unstable.
- Type 6: extra-articular deformity (femoral/tibial): consider osteotomy or navigation/robotic/ patient-specific planning, stems; IM guides unreliable.
- Compare: Ranawat (older) groups: I <10 deg with medial intact, II >10 deg with medial stretched, III severe osseous deformity/ post-osteotomy with incompetent MCL.
- Tag: this complements the varus section; use with skyline view (patellar tracking) in valgus.

**CORRECTION / clarification on "Mullaji classification of valgus knees":**
- Published Mullaji work (Mullaji, Bhoskar, Singh, Haidermota; KSSTA 2022;30:2895-2904, online Nov 2021): "Valgus arthritic knees can be classified into nine phenotypes", a RADIOGRAPHIC (coronal-plane, full-leg film) classification, companion to his 7 varus phenotypes (KSSTA 2022;30:2793). It uses HKA, VCA, mLDFA, aLDFA, MPTA etc. and is aimed at individualising resection planes (kinematic/personalised alignment).

### Valgus knee algorithm (examined under anaesthesia)
**Slide (flowchart):** Valgus knees (EUA) -> CORRECTIBLE (Type 1) -> MEDIAL approach. RIGID -> LATERAL approach -> branches: No associated deformity (Type 2) -> graduated lateral release +/- LEO (distal shift); With fixed flexion deformity (Type 3) -> graduated lateral release +/- LEO (posterior shift); With hyperextension deformity (Type 4) -> minimum distal femur resection; Lax/incompetent MCL (Type 5) -> constrained implant. (Bottom of slide cut off in photo; Type 6 extra-articular not visible.)
**NOTE: numbering differs from the previous slide** (there Type 3 = hyperextension, Type 4 = flexion; here Type 3 = FFD, Type 4 = hyperextension). Treat the flowchart numbering as this slide's own; confirm with speaker.
**Added:**
- Decision point is examination UNDER ANAESTHESIA (muscle spasm gone): correctible vs rigid decides the approach.
- Correctible: standard medial parapatellar approach.
- Rigid: lateral approach (or medial with lateral release) gives direct access to tight lateral structures; trade-off: patellar eversion/closure and extensor mechanism concerns.
- LEO = lateral epicondylar osteotomy (lifts the lateral epicondyle with LCL/popliteus origin, then slides it). Slide direction matters: distal shift opens extension gap (no deformity group), posterior shift opens flexion gap (FFD group). Keeps lateral structures intact versus piecemeal release.
- Graduated lateral release order (standard text): osteophytes, then posterolateral capsule/ITB (extension-tight) or popliteus/LCL (flexion-tight, "pie-crust"), re-check after each step.
- Hyperextension: minimum distal femoral resection to avoid a big extension gap; later consider constraint with a stop.
- Incompetent MCL: constrained implant (CCK/VVC +/- stems), as in varus table.


### Robotic versus conventional: what is optimised? (Lancet RACER-Knee)
**Slide:** Robotics can improve reproducibility of planned component positioning and alignment. It does not optimise diabetes, anaemia, infection, expectations or pain phenotype. No guarantee of better satisfaction, lower infection or longer survivorship for every patient. Compare clinically important outcomes, not only radiographic outliers. Trial cited: The Lancet, Vol 408, Issue 10558, pp 924-934 (5 Sept 2026), "Robotic-arm-assisted versus conventional total knee replacement (RACER-Knee): a pragmatic, multicentre, participant-masked and assessor-masked, superiority, randomised controlled trial" (Parsons, Metcalfe, Griffin, Khatri, Bradley, Blyth et al.). Interpretation on slide: in this pragmatic trial, rTKR as delivered in routine practice was more costly than cTKR and did not provide a clinically meaningful patient benefit at 12 months after randomisation; similar safety (harms) profiles. Margin note: better precision does not equal guaranteed better patient-reported outcome.
**Added:** I have only the slide's interpretation line, not the full paper; get the primary outcome and effect size from the paper before quoting numbers. Take-home: robot = precision tool, not a substitute for patient optimisation (links to the pre-op optimisation section on HbA1c, anaemia, nutrition) or for alignment philosophy decisions (Day 1 afternoon talk).
