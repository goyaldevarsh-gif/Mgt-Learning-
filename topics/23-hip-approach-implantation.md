## 1. Posterolateral approach: flow

:::flow
[teal] Posterolateral (Technique 3.2) | Lateral position, secure pelvis [[p.206-209]]
v
Split gluteus maximus | Detach short external rotators + posterior capsule (tag them)
v
Dislocate, neck cut | Template-based cut above lesser trochanter
v
Expose acetabulum | Retractors, remove labrum, ream
v
Femur prep and trial | Check stability, leg length
v
[amber] Repair | Posterior capsule + rotators to trochanter, tight fascia closure
:::

> [!samajh] Dislocation rate kam karne ka tareeka
> Posterior approach mein **soft-tissue envelope repair** hi main safeguard hai. Book bhi yahi kehti hai: careful posterior reconstruction **dislocation risk greatly reduces** [[p.221]].

## 2. Cup implantation checks

- **Cementless cup (Tech 3.3):** ream to hemisphere, press-fit, screws in the **safe quadrant** (Wasielewski system) [[p.210-212]]
- Target: **inclination 40-45 deg, anteversion ~20 deg**; combined anteversion (cup + stem) **25-40 deg**
- **Cemented cup (Tech 3.4):** keyholes, dry bed, pressurise cement [[p.212-213]]

## 3. Femoral implantation

- **Cementless (Tech 3.5):** ream then broach, trial reduction for stability, fracture is the intra-op risk [[p.214-218]]
- **Cemented (Tech 3.6)** [[p.218-221]]:

:::flow
[teal] Canal prep | Cement **restrictor** plug, pulsatile lavage, dry
v
Cement mix | Vacuum mix (porosity reduction), change gloves
v
Inject at dough phase | Retrograde with gun, **~4 min** (Simplex)
v
Pressurise | Occlusive nozzle, ~30 s
v
Insert stem | **~6 min**, hold still until hard; do not rigidly attach inserter
:::

- Mantle **2-4 mm proximal, 2 mm distal**
- Too runny = blood/debris, weak mantle. Too late = cannot seat the stem
- Fill unfilled centraliser holes (air expands with cement heat)
- Cement-bone interface best with **canal lavage**; lavage also reduces marrow embolisation

## 4. Direct anterior approach (Technique 3.7) [[p.221-225]]

- Distal half of **Smith-Petersen**. **Intermuscular and internervous** (TFL vs sartorius), patient supine
- Incision lateral to the interval (3 cm distal and 3 cm lateral to ASIS) to spare the **LFCN**
- Cauterise ascending branches of the **lateral circumflex vessels**
- Femur elevation is the hard part: figure-of-four, **hyperextension**, release superior capsule, then piriformis and conjoined tendon
- Cup tends to be put in **too much abduction and anteversion** supine
- Interval **cannot extend distally**: use a separate exposure for osteotomy, implant removal, cerclage

![Fig 3.64: Positioning for femoral exposure](figs/h3_64.jpg)

| Evidence (book) | Finding |
|---|---|
| Dislocation | Lower claimed, but gap closed with **large heads + soft tissue repair** |
| Recovery | Faster early (stay, narcotics, aids at 2 wks); **few differences after 6 wks** |
| Dedicated table (Matta, 494 hips) | 1 femoral nerve palsy, 3 trochanter fractures, 2 femoral shaft fractures, 3 ankle fractures |
| Fluoroscopy | Mixed; Rothman (Tischler): no benefit, more time and cost |

> [!flag] Exposure judicious soft-tissue release se karo, **zor se traction aur rotation se nahi**: fractures aur nerve palsy ka rasta yahi hai [[p.224]].

## 5. Minimally invasive THA [[p.225-227]]

- Not one operation: incisions **<= 10 cm** (consensus). Single posterior most common, then single-incision DAA
- Risks: poor exposure, malpositioned implants, loosening, wound problems, unrecognised fractures, nerve injury, leg length error
- Not for obese (BMI > 30), muscular males, revisions, dysplasia, stiff hips. **Never hesitate to lengthen the incision**
- Several RCTs: early gain (less limp, short stay) but **no difference by 1 yr**; others none. Confounded by **rapid rehab protocols**
- Parratte and Pagnano: some muscle damage is unavoidable; **site differs** (anterior: gluteus medius front, TFL; posterior: short external rotators, gluteus minimus)

## 6. Computer-assisted surgery [[p.227-228]]

| System | Based on | Catch |
|---|---|---|
| **Imageless** | Landmarks digitised (ASIS, pubic symphysis) | Errors in large patients; generic bone model |
| **Fluoroscopic** | Multi-angle images | Better for **abduction than anteversion**; radiation, bulky |
| **CT-based** | Pre-op CT virtual model | Patient-specific; needs pre-op CT |

- Navigation reduces **outliers**, not mean; benefit on outcome and survivorship **not yet proven**; cost [[p.228]]
- **Robotic:** CT-based arm assists reaming and cup placement; femur first, then cup version set for correct combined anteversion

## 7. Trochanteric osteotomy [[p.228-231]]

Most THAs now need **no** osteotomy. Indications: ankylosis, severe protrusio, high dislocation DDH, abductor laxity (distal advancement), revision.

![Fig 3.70: Types of trochanteric osteotomy](figs/h3_70.jpg)

| Type | Attachments | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Only abductors (gluteus medius + minimus) | Extensile acetabular exposure, cage, big graft. 45 deg cut, protect **sciatic nerve** |
| **Trochanteric slide** (Glassman/Engh/Bobyn) | Gluteus medius + trochanter + vastus lateralis in continuity | Lower abductor insufficiency; limp less |
| **Extended (ETO)** | Lateral femoral cortex lifted with trochanter | Remove well-fixed stem; **cementless** revision only |

- Reattach with hip abducted **<= 10-15 deg**; anatomic or slight distal position unites within 6 months
- Wires: nonunion up to **25%** reported; **compression** across osteotomy and biplanar (chevron) cut help [[p.230]]

:::qa ETO kab aur kyun sirf cementless ke saath?
Removes well-fixed implants in revision; large cortical segment lost so cementing becomes imperfect, hence only when cementless femoral reconstruction is planned [[p.229]].
:::

:::qa Cemented stem: cement kab inject aur stem kab daalna (Simplex)?
Inject at dough phase ~4 min, insert stem at medium dough ~6 min; timing varies with brand, temperature, humidity [[p.220]].
:::
