## Big picture

Pain on the **little-finger side of the wrist** has many causes. The trick is to **locate the pain precisely** and then use the right test.

## Differential by location {revise}

| Zone | Think of |
|---|---|
| Ulnar fovea (dorsal groove between ulnar styloid and FCU) | **TFCC foveal tear**, ulnotriquetral ligament |
| Dorsal ulnar | ECU tendinitis or subluxation, DRUJ instability |
| Over the ulnar head | **DRUJ** arthritis or instability |
| Between ulna and triquetrum | **Ulnocarpal impingement** (positive ulnar variance) |
| Volar ulnar | FCU tendinitis, pisotriquetral arthritis, hook of hamate fracture |
| Triquetrum / lunate | Lunotriquetral (LT) ligament tear |
| Neurological | Ulnar tunnel (Guyon), dorsal ulnar sensory nerve |

## Focused tests {revise}

- **Ulnar fovea sign**: pressing the soft spot between the styloid and FCU reproduces pain. Sensitive for foveal TFCC tear or ulnotriquetral injury.
- **Press test**: pushing up from a chair with the arm held. Pain suggests TFCC.
- **Ulnocarpal stress test**: ulnar deviation with axial load and forearm rotation. Pain or click suggests TFCC or ulnocarpal impaction.
- **DRUJ ballottement / piano key test**: the radius is stabilised and the ulna is moved dorsal-volar. Excess laxity compared with the other side suggests DRUJ instability.
- **ECU synergy test**: against resisted radial deviation with the thumb, pain suggests ECU.
- **LT ballottement / shear**: pain or clunk between triquetrum and lunate.

> [!verify] Each test's sensitivity and specificity (and exact hand position) vary between sources. Learn the manoeuvre from a hand surgery book.

## TFCC vs DRUJ: the key difference {revise}

| | TFCC | DRUJ |
|---|---|---|
| Pain provoked by | Ulnar deviation + loading, pressing up | End-range forearm rotation |
| Sign | Fovea sign, click | Ballottement, translation of ulna |
| Cause | Trauma or degeneration | Instability after TFCC foveal tear, arthritis, or distal radius malunion |

They overlap, because the foveal fibres of the TFCC are the main stabiliser of the DRUJ.

## Imaging

- PA X-ray in **neutral rotation** to measure **ulnar variance** (positive = ulnocarpal impaction), plus a pronated grip view.
- MRI or MR arthrogram for TFCC, LT ligament and cartilage.
- Wrist arthroscopy is the gold standard for TFCC.
- CT for DRUJ subluxation, with both wrists in pronation, neutral and supination.

## Palmer classification of TFCC

- **Class 1 traumatic**: 1A central perforation, 1B ulnar (peripheral) avulsion, 1C distal (ulnocarpal ligaments), 1D radial avulsion.
- **Class 2 degenerative**: 2A to 2E, grades of wear with ulnocarpal impaction.

## Treatment {revise}

:::flow
[teal] Splint 4 to 6 weeks, NSAID, activity change
v
Steroid injection (diagnostic and therapeutic)
v
[amber] Arthroscopy: debride central tear (1A), repair peripheral tear (1B)
v
[red] Ulnar shortening for positive variance; reconstruction for chronic DRUJ instability
:::

- Central tears (1A) have poor blood supply and are **debrided**.
- Peripheral tears (1B) and foveal tears are **repaired** (good blood supply).
- Positive ulnar variance with impaction: ulnar shortening osteotomy or wafer procedure.

## Viva {viva}

:::qa What is the ulnar fovea sign?
Tenderness in the soft spot between the ulnar styloid and FCU, suggesting foveal TFCC tear.
:::

:::qa Which X-ray detail is crucial?
Ulnar variance on a neutral rotation PA view.
:::

:::qa How do central and peripheral TFCC tears differ in treatment?
Central: debride (avascular). Peripheral: repair (vascular).
:::

:::qa Why do TFCC and DRUJ problems overlap?
The foveal fibres of the TFCC are the main stabilisers of the DRUJ.
:::
