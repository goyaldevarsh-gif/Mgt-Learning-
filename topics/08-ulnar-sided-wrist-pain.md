## 2. History: mechanism points to the structure

| Clue | Think of |
|---|---|
| Fall on pronated outstretched hand, or rotation under axial load | TFCC tear, LT tear, DRUJ injury, triquetral chip |
| Golf, baseball, racquet (handle pressing on hypothenar) | Hook of hamate fracture |
| Sudden forceful supination + ulnar deviation + flexion (tennis, golf) | ECU subluxation |
| Repetitive loaded pronation/ulnar deviation (gymnast, racquet, push-ups, hammering) | Ulnar impaction, degenerative TFCC |
| Click or clunk with rotation or grip | DRUJ instability, LT tear, ECU |
| Pain turning a doorknob or opening a jar | DRUJ or TFCC |
| Ring/little finger numbness, cold intolerance (cyclist, mechanic, karate) | Guyon's canal, ulnar artery (hypothenar hammer) |
| Night pain, fever, mass, known RA | Red flags, section 4 |

## 3. Examination

1. Inspect both wrists: swelling, ulnar head prominence (dorsal subluxation), ganglion, ECU swelling.
2. ROM and grip: painful pronosupination, crepitus.
3. **One-finger test:** palpate all 8 points on the map one by one.
4. Provocative tests (no single test is diagnostic, look for a cluster):

| Test | How | Suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Ulnar fovea sign | Press the soft spot between ulnar styloid, FCU tendon, volar ulnar head and pisiform | Foveal TFCC tear or ulnotriquetral injury (Tay: ~95% sensitive, ~86% specific, flag) |
| Piano key / DRUJ ballottement | Stabilise radius, translate ulnar head dorsal-volar, in neutral, pronation, supination. Compare sides | DRUJ instability (soft end-point, side difference) |
| TFCC load (ulnar grind) | Ulnar deviation + axial load + pronate/supinate | Pain or click: TFCC tear, impaction |
| Press test | Push up from a chair with palms | Ulnar pain: TFCC (sensitive, not specific) |
| LT ballottement (Reagan), Kleinman shear | Stabilise lunate, shuck or shear the triquetrum/pisiform | LT tear (pain, laxity, click) |
| ECU subluxation | Move wrist from pronation to supination with flexion and ulnar deviation, look and feel for snapping | ECU subsheath tear |
| ECU synergy test | Resisted radial thumb abduction, forearm supinated (exact technique: check Ruland) | ECU tendinopathy |
| Pisotriquetral grind | Compress and glide pisiform on triquetrum | Pisotriquetral arthritis |
| Hook of hamate | Tender ~1 cm distal-radial to pisiform. Pull test: resisted ring/little flexion, wrist ulnar-deviated | Hamate hook fracture |
| Allen test, Tinel at Guyon | Standard | Ulnar artery, ulnar nerve |

## 4. Investigations (in this order)

1. **X-rays**
   - PA in neutral rotation (shoulder abducted 90°, elbow 90°): ulnar variance, DRUJ, ulnar styloid.
   - PA pronated grip view: variance increases ~1-2 mm dynamically (flag). Isse hidden impaction pakadta hai.
   - True lateral: DISI/VISI, dorsal triquetral chip (best seen here), DRUJ subluxation.
   - 30° supinated oblique: pisotriquetral joint. Carpal tunnel view: hamate hook (often misses, CT better).
2. **Ultrasound:** dynamic ECU subluxation, tenosynovitis, ganglion. OPD mein cheap aur quick.
3. **MRI:** TFCC (3T), lunate/ulnar head edema (impaction), occult fractures, tendons. **MR arthrography** is better for peripheral/foveal TFCC and LT tears. Caution: degenerative TFCC changes are common in asymptomatic older wrists, so correlate clinically.
4. **CT:** hook of hamate, triquetrum, DRUJ. For instability, get bilateral CT in neutral, full pronation, full supination and compare with the normal side.
5. **Diagnostic injection:** lidocaine into the DRUJ, ulnocarpal joint or ECU sheath. Relief localises the source.
6. **Wrist arthroscopy:** gold standard for TFCC and LT, and also therapeutic.
7. As needed: NCS/EMG (Guyon, cubital tunnel), Doppler or CT angiography (ulnar artery), ESR/CRP/RA/uric acid if inflammatory.

## 5. Diagnoses

**TFCC tears, Palmer classification**

| Class | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1A | Central perforation | Avascular, debride |
| 1B | Ulnar (peripheral/foveal) avulsion, ± ulnar styloid fracture | DRUJ instability possible, repairable |
| 1C | Distal (ulnocarpal ligament) avulsion | |
| 1D | Radial avulsion from sigmoid notch | |
| 2A | TFCC wear (degenerative) | Impaction continuum |
| 2B | + lunate/ulnar head chondromalacia | |
| 2C | + TFCC perforation | |
| 2D | + LT ligament tear | |
| 2E | + ulnocarpal and DRUJ arthritis | |

Atzei refined foveal tears (class 1 distal, 2 complete, 3 proximal repairable, 4 irreparable, 5 DRUJ arthritis, flag). Treatment choice ispe depend karta hai.

**Other causes at a glance**

| Diagnosis | Clue | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Ulnar impaction | Positive (or dynamic positive) variance, pain with grip/ulnar deviation | MRI: ulnar lunate edema. X-ray: cysts, sclerosis |
| LT tear | Dorsal LT gap tender, ballottement positive | VISI in advanced cases |
| ECU tendinitis or subluxation | Tender over groove, snapping | Dynamic US helps |
| FCU tendinitis | Pain at pisiform with resisted flexion/ulnar deviation | Calcific tendinitis is possible |
| Pisotriquetral arthritis | Positive grind test | Supinated oblique view |
| Hook of hamate fracture | Hypothenar pain, grip pain | CT. Risks: ulnar nerve, little finger FDP rupture |
| Triquetral dorsal chip | Fall on extended wrist | Lateral X-ray |
| DRUJ instability/arthritis | Piano key, pain on rotation | CT in rotation, compare sides |
| Guyon's canal syndrome | Ring/little numbness, weakness | NCS, US/MRI for mass |
| Hypothenar hammer (ulnar artery) | Cold, pale, painful fingers | Allen test, Doppler/angiography |

**Red flags (don't miss):** Galeazzi (radial shaft fracture + DRUJ), Essex-Lopresti (radial head + DRUJ + membrane, never excise the radial head), infection, tumour, RA (caput ulnae, extensor tendon rupture at ulnar head), gout/CPPD, Kienböck, referred pain (cubital tunnel, C8).

## 6. Acute ulnar-sided wrist pain (recent injury, roughly up to 6 weeks)

1. **Safety check:** open wound, neurovascular status, forearm and elbow (Galeazzi, Essex-Lopresti).
2. **X-ray series.** Fracture ya DRUJ dislocation mile to usko treat karo:
   - Nondisplaced triquetral dorsal chip: short-arm splint/cast ~3-6 weeks.
   - Hook of hamate (CT confirm): nondisplaced acute fracture can be casted ~6 weeks, but union is unreliable. Symptomatic nonunion mein hook excision.
   - DRUJ dislocation: reduce and immobilise in the position that holds it reduced (supination for dorsal ulnar head, pronation for volar, flag to verify).
   - Ulnar styloid fracture: usually only matters if it is a base fracture with DRUJ instability (foveal avulsion). Associated distal radius fracture mein styloid routinely fix nahi karte, sirf radius fix hone ke baad DRUJ unstable ho tab.
3. **X-ray normal:** check DRUJ stability and compare with the other side.
   - Unstable DRUJ: early CT/MRI and hand surgery referral.
   - Stable DRUJ + tender fovea + positive TFCC load: treat as a TFCC sprain or peripheral tear. Splint (short-arm or Muenster-type, which limits pronosupination) for ~4-6 weeks (flag), finger ROM from day 1, analgesia.
   - Snapping at the ulnar groove: acute ECU subluxation. Cast or Muenster-type with forearm pronated, wrist extended and radially deviated for ~6 weeks (flag). Recurs often, then subsheath repair.
4. **Still painful at 6 weeks,** or mechanical symptoms or instability earlier: MRI/MR arthrogram (+ CT for DRUJ). Occult fractures (triquetrum, hamate, scaphoid) bhi recheck karo.
5. No steroid injection in the acute phase.

## 7. Chronic ulnar-sided wrist pain (more than ~3 months)

1. Identify the structure: one-finger point, provocative tests, X-ray variance.
2. **Conservative trial ~3 months:** activity modification (avoid loaded pronosupination and ulnar deviation), splint or wrist support, NSAIDs, hand therapy, and a steroid injection that is both diagnostic and therapeutic (ulnocarpal, DRUJ or ECU sheath). Avoid repeated injections near tendons (rupture risk).
3. MRI or MR arthrogram (+ CT if DRUJ instability).
4. Failed conservative care: wrist arthroscopy, diagnostic and therapeutic.

**Definitive treatment by diagnosis**

| Diagnosis | Options |
|---|---|
| Central TFCC tear (1A), stable DRUJ | Arthroscopic debridement, preserve the peripheral ~2 mm rim |
| Peripheral/foveal tear with DRUJ instability or failed conservative care | Arthroscopic or open repair (foveal reattachment). Irreparable: tendon graft reconstruction |
| Ulnar impaction, mild positive variance | Wafer procedure (arthroscopic or open), roughly up to 2-4 mm resection (flag) |
| Ulnar impaction, larger variance, or TFCC/DRUJ involvement | Ulnar shortening osteotomy |
| DRUJ arthritis | Darrach, Sauvé-Kapandji, hemiresection interposition, ulnar head prosthesis |
| LT tear | Partial: debride ± pinning. Complete: repair ± reconstruction. VISI or arthritis: LT fusion |
| ECU tendinitis | Splint, NSAID, sheath injection. Surgery rarely |
| ECU subluxation (chronic) | Subsheath repair or reconstruction (extensor retinaculum slip) |
| Pisotriquetral arthritis | Splint, injection, then pisiformectomy |
| Hook of hamate nonunion | Excision |
| Guyon's canal | Decompression, treat the cause (ganglion, fracture) |
| Ulnar artery thrombosis | Vascular referral, stop trauma/smoking |

**Rehab (tera favourite angle):**
- Phases: immobilise (~4-6 weeks if repaired or sprained), then ROM, then strengthening (~8-12 weeks), then return to racquet/weights ~3-4 months (flag).
- Train the dynamic DRUJ stabilisers: ECU, FCU and pronator quadratus.
- Grip work in neutral forearm first. Loaded pronosupination and ulnar deviation last.
- Taping or a wrist support for return to sport. Technique correction (racquet grip, handlebar position, lifting form) rukavat nahi, root cause hota hai.

## 8. OPD checklist: 5 questions {revise}

1. Acute (fall/twist) or chronic (repetitive)?
2. Exactly where? One-finger test on all 8 points.
3. Is the DRUJ stable? Compare with the other side.
4. X-ray: fracture? ulnar variance? Grip view?
5. Which cluster? Then pick the next test (MR arthrogram, CT, US) instead of ordering MRI blindly.

## DRUJ aur TFCC: alag hain kya?

DRUJ aur TFCC **alag cheezein** hain, par aapas mein bahut closely linked hain.

**DRUJ (distal radioulnar joint)** ek **joint** hai. Ulna ka head radius ke sigmoid notch mein baitha hai, aur yahin pronation-supination hota hai.

**TFCC** ek **soft tissue complex** hai (disc + ligaments) jo ulna aur carpus ke beech aur radius-ulna ke beech phaila hai. Iske parts: central disc, dorsal aur volar radioulnar ligaments (deep fibres ulnar fovea pe attach), ulnocarpal ligaments, ulnar collateral ligament, meniscus homologue, ECU subsheath.

| | DRUJ | TFCC |
|---|---|---|
| Kya hai | Joint (bone + cartilage + capsule) | Cartilage-ligament complex |
| Kaam | Forearm rotation | DRUJ ko stable rakhna + ulnocarpal shock absorber |
| Problem | Arthritis, instability, dislocation | Tear (central, peripheral, foveal) |

**Link:** Bone se DRUJ bahut shallow fit hai (sigmoid notch ulna head se zyada chhota/flat hai). Isliye uski stability **soft tissue** se aati hai, aur sabse bada contributor TFCC ke radioulnar ligaments hain, khaas kar **foveal (deep) fibres**. Dusre stabilisers: DRUJ capsule, pronator quadratus (dynamic), ECU subsheath, interosseous membrane.

**Clinical matlab:**
- **Peripheral/foveal TFCC tear** (Palmer 1B) se **DRUJ instability** ho sakti hai (piano key positive).
- **Central TFCC tear** (1A) mein DRUJ aksar stable rehta hai.
- **DRUJ arthritis** (sigmoid notch/ulna head ka cartilage ghisna) TFCC tear ke bina bhi ho sakta hai, ya late stage mein dono saath (Palmer 2E).
- Exam mein dono alag dhoondo: DRUJ ke liye **ballottement/piano key** (stability) aur **pain with rotation + compression** (arthritis). TFCC ke liye **ulnar fovea sign, TFCC load test, press test**.

**Ek line mein:** DRUJ = joint, TFCC = us joint ka main ligament-cartilage stabiliser (plus ulnocarpal cushion). TFCC phatta to DRUJ dheela ho sakta hai, isliye dono ko saath check karte hain.
